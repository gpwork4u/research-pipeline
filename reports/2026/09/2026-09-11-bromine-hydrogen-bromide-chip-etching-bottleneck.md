# 一句話結論

South Korea's 97.5% dependence on Israeli-origin bromine for memory-chip etch gas is a real single point of failure, but 2026's price spike already reversed without disrupting output.

# Executive Summary

Bromine, converted into semiconductor-grade hydrogen bromide (HBr), is the etch chemical used to carve transistor structures into every DRAM, NAND and HBM chip -- and South Korea imports roughly 97.5% of its bromine from Israel. A cluster of 2026 trade-press and policy analysis flagged this concentration as a chip-industry risk potentially worse than the helium shortage this pipeline covered in August. But the two largest listed bromine producers, ICL Group and Albemarle, both confirmed in their Q2 2026 earnings calls that prices peaked in April 2026 and have since normalized. No source found shows any actual DRAM, NAND or HBM output impact at Samsung, SK Hynix or Micron -- SK Hynix instead posted record, AI-driven revenue growth of 257% year-over-year. The underlying concentration risk is real and durable, but the 2026 stress test suggests it is not yet a binding constraint on AI memory supply.

# Research Question / Why Now

**Primary question:** see front matter above.

**Sub-questions:** How concentrated is bromine/HBr production, and how exposed is Korea's memory-fab base? What triggered the 2026 price spike, and has it eased? Have Samsung, SK Hynix or Micron disclosed any output impact? Can supply be diversified away from Israel/Jordan quickly? What would convert this from latent risk to a binding constraint?

**Why now:** A September 9, 2026 trade piece on bromine-supplier diversification follows a run of 2026 disclosures no prior report in this pipeline examined: TrendForce (March 9, April 17) and Digitimes (April 15) flagged Korea's 97.5% bromine-import dependency on Israel; War on the Rocks (April 23) and gasworld (April 29) described the risk as potentially exceeding the helium shortage. Crucially, ICL Group's (~Aug. 5) and Albemarle's (~Aug. 13) Q2 2026 earnings now show prices already normalizing -- a live test of whether the alarm became an actual bottleneck.

**Thesis:** The Israel-bromine concentration meets the strict Structural Bottleneck test on the geographic-exposure framing, but 2026's real-world stress test shows it has not yet bound AI memory output.

- Because SK Hynix posted record revenue with no disclosed bromine impact, and no output disruption was found anywhere, the 97.5% statistic describes exposure, not a proven binding constraint. _(evidence: ev-9)_

# Core Mechanism

Every DRAM, NAND and HBM chip is etched using plasma processes that rely on hydrogen bromide (HBr) gas, refined from raw bromine. Roughly two-thirds of global bromine production traces to a handful of brine deposits in Israel and Jordan, and South Korea -- home to Samsung and SK Hynix, the world's dominant memory producers -- sources about 97.5% of its bromine from Israel alone, much of it converted on-site at a single Dead Sea complex operated by ICL Group.

As AI-driven demand for HBM, DRAM and NAND accelerated through 2026, and as advanced nodes raised per-wafer etch-step counts, 2026 Middle East tensions triggered fears that a Dead Sea disruption could ration scarce HBr toward the highest-value line (HBM) at the expense of commodity memory. Bromine prices did spike, peaking above $6,000/tonne in April 2026. But unlike this pipeline's confirmed helium and rare-earth bottlenecks -- where suppliers themselves describe unresolved, multi-year tightness -- both ICL and Albemarle's own Q2 2026 results show prices already normalizing, and Korea's memory makers reported record output with no disclosed input impact. The concentration risk remains extreme and largely unaddressed, but it has not yet converted into a binding constraint.

# Final Assessment

**Verdict: VALIDATE** (confidence: medium)

Korea's 97.5% dependence on Israeli-origin bromine for semiconductor-grade HBr meets the strict Structural Bottleneck test on the geographic-concentration framing -- high demand, low near-term elasticity for this specific import relationship, and hard substitutability. But 2026 supplied a real stress test: a Middle East-tension-driven price spike that peaked in April has, by both major producers' own account, already normalized, and no source confirms any DRAM, NAND or HBM output impact. This is a genuine, underappreciated single point of failure worth continued monitoring, but the evidence does not yet support treating it as a currently binding constraint on AI memory supply.
