# One-Sentence Conclusion

SUMCO's deliberate restraint on new 300mm wafer capacity looks like a structural bottleneck at the oligopoly's core, but Shin-Etsu, GlobalWafers, SK Siltron and Siltronic are all expanding, so the constraint likely shows up as price, not shortage.

# Executive Summary

Every AI logic chip and HBM stack starts as a 300mm silicon wafer, and the two largest suppliers -- Shin-Etsu and SUMCO, roughly 53-54% of that market -- are behaving very differently. SUMCO indefinitely shelved a JPY225 billion, two-fab expansion in Saga Prefecture in March 2026, explicitly to prioritize AI demand over phone chips, while Shin-Etsu is expanding 300mm capacity over 20% by year-end. GlobalWafers, SK Siltron and Siltronic are all also adding capacity, and China's NSIG grew 300mm shipments over 90% in H1 2026. UBS estimated on September 18, 2026 that wafer makers need 40-50% price increases to justify new capacity, against actual 2026 chip-price hikes of only 3-10% -- suggesting the market has not yet reached scarcity pricing, and that further price increases are more likely than an outright shortage.

# Research Question / Why Now

**Primary question:** see front matter above.

**Sub-questions:** How fast is AI-linked wafer demand growing relative to supply? Why is SUMCO restraining capacity while its peers expand? What does UBS's 40-50% price-increase threshold imply about supply elasticity? How hard is it to substitute leading-edge wafer supply? What would confirm the bottleneck is binding AI chip output, not just cost?

**Why now:** UBS's September 18, 2026 note on the price increase needed to justify new wafer capacity, SK Hynix's roughly 104% jump in wafer purchases, and SUMCO's Nikkei-reported indefinite fab delay all cluster in the past three weeks, against a backdrop of simultaneous capacity expansion elsewhere in the industry.

**Thesis:** 300mm silicon wafers show early signs of a structural bottleneck at the oligopoly's core, but aggressive expansion elsewhere means it is more likely to express itself as sustained price increases than as a shortage constraining AI chip output.

# Known Facts

- SUMCO indefinitely delayed two planned 300mm fabs (a combined JPY225 billion project) to prioritize AI demand over phone chips; Japan's METI cut its subsidy from up to JPY75B to JPY19.3B. _(s5, s6)_
- SUMCO's Q2 FY2026 guidance still points to higher sales and a narrowing loss on higher 300mm volumes and strong AI logic/HBM/NAND demand. _(s4)_
- Shin-Etsu's Q1 FY2026 sales rose 5.4% YoY; it raised its FY2027 profit forecast 11% and targets over 20% more 300mm capacity by end-2026. _(s7)_
- Shin-Etsu and SUMCO together hold roughly 53-54% of global 300mm wafer supply. _(s8)_
- Micron committed $500 million in financing and a 10-year supply deal to GlobalWafers' Sherman, Texas 300mm plant. _(s2)_
- GlobalWafers' Q2 2026 operating margin fell to 9.4% from 15.2% a year earlier while ramping new capacity; a Novara, Italy fire will weigh on Q3. _(s3)_
- SK Siltron started a new Gumi, Korea 300mm plant (2.3 trillion won) in July 2026, described as "firing up capacity as rivals pause." _(s15, s16)_
- Siltronic's CEO cited AI as a long-term growth driver, with 300mm utilization staying high through 2026. _(s1)_
- China's NSIG grew 300mm shipments over 90% and revenue 36.5% YoY in H1 2026, nearing 1.2 million wafers/month. _(s17)_
- UBS estimates wafer makers need 40-50% price increases to justify new capacity; 300mm demand growth is projected at 11%/14%/13% for 2026/27/28. _(s13)_
- SK Hynix's wafer purchases jumped roughly 104% as it moved early to secure supply. _(s10)_
- 2026 chip-level price increases (TSMC 3-10%, Samsung ~10%, SK Hynix equipment 3-4%) remain well below UBS's 40-50% threshold. _(s11, s12)_
- SEMI's official data shows wafer shipments up 13.1% YoY in Q1 2026 and 7.4% in Q2 2026, attributed largely to AI demand. _(s14, s20)_

# Reasonable Inferences

- Because SUMCO restrains capacity while four other major producers expand, supply elasticity looks low at the oligopoly's core but higher at its margins -- the bottleneck likely shows up as price, not shortage. _(s5, s6, s7, s2, s15, s1)_
- Actual 2026 price hikes (3-10%) sitting well below UBS's 40-50% threshold suggests pricing hasn't yet triggered broad capacity investment, implying further increases are likely before supply responds. _(s13, s11, s12)_
- NSIG's rapid growth is not yet reported as qualified for advanced logic/HBM specs, so it likely relieves Chinese and mid-tier demand before substituting for AI-grade wafers elsewhere. _(s17)_
- AI is a fast-growing minority (~12%) of wafer volume, consistent with a marginal segment setting the price for the whole market. _(s18)_

# Unknowns

# Core Mechanism

Every AI accelerator and HBM stack begins as a bare 300mm silicon wafer, and qualifying a new wafer supplier for a leading-edge logic or memory process takes years -- so buyers cannot easily switch suppliers even as demand surges. Shin-Etsu and SUMCO's combined 53-54% share of that market gives them real pricing power, and SUMCO has now demonstrated it: rather than build the capacity it originally planned, it shelved a JPY225 billion project and let AI-linked demand absorb existing leading-edge output at firmer prices.

But that discipline is not industry-wide. Shin-Etsu, GlobalWafers, SK Siltron and Siltronic are all simultaneously investing in new 300mm capacity, and China's NSIG is scaling fast domestically. UBS's finding that a 40-50% price increase is needed to justify new capacity -- against actual chip-level increases of only 3-10% so far -- suggests the market is still working through a gap between rising demand and the price signal needed to unlock a broader supply response, rather than approaching a hard ceiling on output.

# Strongest Bear Case

- **Supply**: Four of five major producers are actively adding 300mm capacity in 2026, pushing the blended market toward Medium elasticity.
- **Demand**: AI is still only ~12% of 300mm wafer volume; a PC/smartphone slowdown could free capacity for AI without new fabs.
- **Economics**: Actual 2026 price increases (3-10%) remain far below the 40-50% UBS says is needed for new capacity -- real scarcity pricing hasn't arrived.
- **Competition**: China's NSIG is scaling 300mm output over 90% YoY with state backing, a long-run release valve at least for domestic demand.
- **Execution**: GlobalWafers' margin compression while expanding shows new capacity is costly, which could simply be a temporary ramp cost, not proof of a durable bottleneck.

# Final Assessment

**Verdict: VALIDATE** (confidence: medium)

The evidence supports a genuine, if partial, structural bottleneck: SUMCO's deliberate capacity restraint, Shin-Etsu and SUMCO's combined 53-54% share, and UBS's finding that current pricing sits well below the level needed to justify new capacity all point toward a durable leading-edge constraint. But the fact that Shin-Etsu, GlobalWafers, SK Siltron and Siltronic are simultaneously expanding, and that AI remains a minority of total wafer volume, means this looks more like a slow-building price bottleneck than an output-constraining shortage today. VALIDATE, not BUILD NOW or PAUSE: the next two quarters of earnings calls and pricing data should show whether SUMCO's restraint or its peers' expansion wins out.
