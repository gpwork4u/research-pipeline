# 一句話結論

Broadcom's Q3 beat confirms CoWoS packaging is easing, but evidence is not yet enough to prove design-partner concentration is a new structural bottleneck.

# Executive Summary

Broadcom's Q3 FY2026 results (reported September 2, 2026) beat the AI semiconductor revenue guide this pipeline tracked on its watchlist, hitting $16.7 billion (+221% YoY), and management introduced unprecedented FY2027/FY2028 targets of $115 billion and $230 billion. TSMC's CoWoS gap is separately reported narrowing from ~20% to 10% by end-2026 on a ~10x capacity build-out since 2023, and OpenAI's first Broadcom-designed chip shipped in Q3 without a cited packaging delay -- together downgrading packaging from Structural Bottleneck to Growth Area, as prior reports anticipated. What has not eased is customer concentration: Broadcom's multi-gigawatt commitments run through six hyperscale customers and almost exclusively Broadcom's own design capability. That is a material bear-case risk, but Broadcom's own guidance implies rapidly scaling supply and Jalapeno shipped on schedule -- so the evidence does not yet prove design capacity itself is a scarce bottleneck rather than an unresolved risk.

# Research Question / Why Now

**Primary question:** see front matter above.

**Sub-questions:**
- How much did Broadcom's actual Q3 FY2026 results and forward guidance exceed the original CoWoS-thesis assumptions from August 2026?
- What does TSMC's reported CoWoS gap-narrowing and OSAT outsourcing imply about whether packaging remains the binding constraint?
- Did OpenAI's first custom accelerator ship without packaging-related delay, and what does that reveal about where the real constraint sits?
- How concentrated is Broadcom's AI backlog among its six disclosed hyperscale customers?
- Is Broadcom's own ASIC design and rack-integration capacity becoming a structurally scarce resource in its own right?

**Why now:** Broadcom reported Q3 FY2026 results on September 2, 2026 that beat the ~$16 billion AI semiconductor revenue guide this pipeline's August 19, 2026 CoWoS-packaging report placed on its watchlist, and disclosed that OpenAI's first custom chip shipped in Q3 without a packaging-related delay -- directly resolving two open watchlist events from that report.

**Thesis:** Broadcom's results beat the original guide and introduced $115B/$230B FY27/FY28 targets, while TSMC's CoWoS gap is reported narrowing toward 10% and OpenAI's chip shipped on schedule -- evidence packaging specifically no longer meets the strict Structural Bottleneck test and is better classified a Growth Area. What has not eased is that the backlog runs through six customers and almost exclusively Broadcom's own design capability -- a material bear-case risk, but not yet evidenced as a proven Structural Bottleneck at the design layer, since Broadcom's own guidance implies rapidly scaling supply and Jalapeno shipped without design-related delay.

# Known Facts

- Broadcom's Q3 FY2026 AI semiconductor revenue grew 221% YoY and 54% QoQ to $16.7 billion, beating the ~$16 billion guide; total revenue was $29.6 billion, up 86% YoY. _(evidence: ev-1, ev-2, ev-3)_
- Broadcom guided Q4 FY2026 AI revenue to $21.7 billion (+236% YoY) and raised full-year guidance to $58 billion (+186% YoY), plus FY2027/FY2028 targets of ~$115B and ~$230B; Hock Tan said supply is secured even as demand outpaces outlook. _(evidence: ev-1, ev-4, ev-5)_
- Broadcom's AI backlog, first cited at $73 billion (18-month window) in December 2025, continues growing across six customers: Google, Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic, ByteDance, Fujitsu. _(evidence: ev-5, ev-7)_
- OpenAI's Jalapeño chip, unveiled June 24, 2026, shipped in Q3 FY2026 continuing into Q4, with no packaging-related delay reported. _(evidence: ev-9, ev-10, ev-11, ev-7)_
- Anthropic committed ~$21 billion via Broadcom for ~1 million Google TPU ("Ironwood") units, over 1GW online in 2026 plus ~3.5GW from 2027; set to become Broadcom's top XPU customer by FY2027. _(evidence: ev-12, ev-13, ev-14, ev-8)_
- Broadcom disclosed a 1.3GW OpenAI 2027 deployment within a 10GW deal by 2029, and a Meta MTIA deal for 3GW through 2028; its 8-K lists customer concentration as a risk factor, and Anthropic/OpenAI are expected to overtake Google as top two XPU customers by FY2027-2028. _(evidence: ev-15, ev-2)_
- TSMC's CoWoS capacity grew roughly 10x from ~13,000 wafers/month (end-2023) to a targeted 120,000-130,000 (end-2026); the supply-demand gap is reported narrowing from ~20% to ~10% by end-2026. _(evidence: ev-16, ev-17)_
- At SEMICON Taiwan 2026, TSMC disclosed equipment demand nearly doubled (1.9x Dec-2025 baseline) in nine months while building ~20 new fabs/packaging plants. _(evidence: ev-18)_

# Reasonable Inferences

- Because the CoWoS gap is narrowing and Jalapeño shipped without packaging delay, TSMC packaging capacity no longer appears the single binding constraint identified in August 2026. _(evidence: ev-16, ev-7, ev-9)_
- Because FY27/FY28 guidance depends on six customers and effectively one dominant design partner, concentration is a material and possibly growing risk, though whether it rises to a proven Structural Bottleneck cannot yet be determined. _(evidence: ev-15, ev-7)_
- Broadcom's own guidance -- scaling from ~$58B (FY26) to ~$230B (FY28), roughly 4x in two years -- is itself evidence of high, not low, supply elasticity at the design layer, weighing against a Structural Bottleneck classification there. _(evidence: ev-4)_
- Because Jalapeño shipped on its announced schedule with no design-related delay cited, this is direct evidence against, not for, a currently binding design/integration constraint at Broadcom. _(evidence: ev-9, ev-10, ev-11)_
- Because TSMC is outsourcing growing CoWoS volume to OSAT partners, headline wafer-capacity figures may overstate readily usable, TSMC-quality supply if outsourced yield lags. _(evidence: ev-17)_

# Core Mechanism

Broadcom pairs multi-year custom-ASIC co-development contracts with hyperscale AI labs against physical capacity from TSMC and, increasingly, OSAT partners. The August 2026 thesis identified CoWoS packaging as the binding constraint reinforcing the largest buyers' positions. Since then, TSMC's reported gap has narrowed toward 10% on a ~10x capacity build-out, and OpenAI's first chip shipped on schedule with no packaging delay -- signs the packaging layer has loosened to a Growth Area. Broadcom's guidance depends on six customers, three of which rotate the top revenue position through 2028, and each new multi-gigawatt commitment routes through Broadcom as sole design partner -- a real concentration risk. But Broadcom's own guidance implies design/shipment capacity scaling ~4x FY26-FY28, and Jalapeño's on-schedule shipment shows no design-side rationing. The evidence supports "packaging eased, concentration risk remains" more than "the bottleneck moved to design capacity."

# Bottleneck Analysis

The strict test is applied one layer upstream of packaging; the evidence does not yet clear the bar there.

| Node | Demand | Supply elasticity | Substitutability | Pricing power | Opportunity | Classification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TSMC CoWoS / advanced-packaging capacity | Extreme | Medium | Medium | Medium | 3 | Growth Area |
| Custom AI ASIC co-design & integration capacity | Extreme | Medium | Hard | High | 3 | Growth Area |

- **CoWoS packaging** is downgraded from the August 2026 Structural Bottleneck call: the gap is narrowing (20% to 10%) and capacity has grown ~10x since 2023, partly via OSAT outsourcing.
- **Custom ASIC design/integration** demand is extreme and concentrated in six customers, but does not meet the strict test: Broadcom's own guidance implies ~4x supply scaling FY26-FY28 and Jalapeño shipped on schedule -- no wait-times, rejected customers, or delayed tape-outs were found. Concentration remains a bear-case risk, not (yet) a proven Structural Bottleneck.

# Strongest Bear Case

- **Execution**: Jalapeño reportedly remains at "small prototype" scale per Hock Tan; scaling to 10-gigawatt, 2029-agreement volumes is unproven.
- **Supply**: The CoWoS gap, while narrowing, is still projected at ~10% by end-2026 -- not closed.

# Final Assessment

**Verdict: VALIDATE** (confidence: medium)

Broadcom's Q3 beat and new $115B/$230B targets confirm the demand side of the original CoWoS thesis was, if anything, understated. The evidence gathered since -- a narrowing gap, ~10x packaging capacity growth, and an on-schedule OpenAI shipment -- shows packaging no longer meets the strict Structural Bottleneck test and downgrades to a Growth Area. Customer and design-partner concentration is a real bear-case risk, but the same evidence -- Broadcom's own scaling guidance and Jalapeño's on-schedule shipment -- lacks the direct scarcity signals (wait-times, rejected customers, delayed tape-outs) a proven Structural Bottleneck needs. The hedged finding is "packaging eased, concentration risk open," not a confirmed migration, so continued validation rather than a stronger call is warranted.
