# 一句話結論

HALEU nuclear fuel is a genuine enrichment-layer bottleneck, but it gates AI's 2028-2035 advanced-reactor bet, not power under construction today.

# Executive Summary

Meta, Google, Amazon and Microsoft have together signed more nuclear power for AI data centers in 18 months than the US signed in the prior twenty years. Much of it -- Oklo's Aurora, TerraPower's Natrium, X-energy's Xe-100, Kairos's KP-FHR -- requires HALEU, a fuel enriched 5-20% that only one small US cascade (Centrus Energy) currently produces commercially, after a 2024 law banned the historically dominant Russian supplier. TerraPower's Natrium has already slipped from a 2028 to a 2031 target partly because of HALEU unavailability. Yet the AI buildout's actual nearest-term nuclear additions -- Constellation's Three Mile Island restart for Microsoft and Vistra's existing plants for Meta -- run on conventional fuel untouched by the shortage. A $2.7 billion DOE program and three separate enrichment build-outs (Centrus, General Matter, Urenco) are racing to close the gap on roughly the same timeline the advanced reactors need fuel.

# Research Question / Why Now

**Primary question:** see front matter above.

**Sub-questions:** How concentrated is HALEU enrichment, and how do DOE's award and the Centrus-Radiant contract change the timeline? Which reactor designs need HALEU versus conventional fuel? What evidence shows HALEU has already delayed a project? How does the 2028 Russian import ban interact with supply? Do demand forecasts outrun realistic capacity before 2030-2035?

**Why now:** On September 9, 2026, Centrus -- the sole US company with an operating commercial HALEU cascade -- signed a supply contract with microreactor developer Radiant, including prepayments to fund capacity expansion. This follows DOE's January 2026 award of $2.7 billion across three enrichers, Centrus completing a 900-kilogram demonstration milestone in mid-June 2026, and General Matter breaking ground on a Kentucky facility in August 2026 -- none previously examined by this pipeline.

**Thesis:** HALEU enrichment meets the strict Structural Bottleneck test, but it binds a future reactor pipeline, not the nuclear power AI is actually building today.

# Reasonable Inferences

- Because Three Mile Island and Vistra's plants use conventional fuel, the current HALEU shortage does not bind the AI power capacity being built through 2027-2028. _(ev-9, ev-11, ev-12)_
- Because every HALEU-dependent reactor targets 2027-2035 and TerraPower's own slip shows the effect is schedule delay, not today's AI compute constraint. _(ev-8, ev-9, ev-10)_
- Because current US HALEU capacity (~900kg to date, 12 MT/year not before 2029) trails even the low end of 2030 demand estimates, a supply gap looks likely regardless of the $2.7B program. _(ev-1, ev-2, ev-14)_
- Because three independent entities (Centrus, General Matter, Urenco) are building capacity with government backing, the shortage is drawing an active capital response, not going unaddressed. _(ev-2, ev-5, ev-15)_
- Because General Matter holds a $900M award without ever operating a facility, near-term credible supply growth still rests mainly on Centrus's proven technology. _(ev-5)_

# Core Mechanism

Advanced reactor designs -- sodium-cooled fast reactors (TerraPower Natrium), high-temperature gas reactors (X-energy Xe-100), fluoride-salt-cooled reactors (Kairos KP-FHR) and Oklo's compact fast reactor -- need fuel enriched to 5-20% U-235, well above the sub-5% used by conventional plants like Three Mile Island. Until 2024, Russia's Rosatom was the only commercial HALEU supplier; a US law now bans Russian imports outright from 2028, leaving Centrus Energy's small Ohio cascade as the sole operating US commercial source.

Hyperscalers have bet heavily on these designs for 2028-2035 AI power, but TerraPower's own experience shows the fuel gap translates into multi-year schedule slips, not capacity that simply appears late. Meanwhile, the nuclear megawatts actually being added before 2028 -- Three Mile Island, Vistra's uprated plants -- sidestep the constraint entirely by using existing, conventionally fueled reactors. DOE's $2.7 billion program, Centrus's expansion, General Matter's Kentucky plant and Urenco's UK line are all racing to build HALEU capacity on roughly the timeline the advanced reactors will need it, making this a bottleneck in a build-out race rather than a settled shortfall.

# Final Assessment

**Verdict: VALIDATE** (confidence: medium)

HALEU enrichment meets the strict Structural Bottleneck test: extreme, law-mandated demand growth; low near-term elasticity from a single small US producer after Russia's exit; and hard substitutability for the reactor designs that need it. But the AI buildout's actual near-term nuclear power -- Three Mile Island, Vistra's uprated plants -- sidesteps it entirely with conventional fuel, and a well-capitalized three-way capacity race is timed to roughly match when the HALEU-dependent reactors will actually need fuel. This is a real constraint on a 2028-2035 bet, not yet a proven constraint on AI compute coming online today.
