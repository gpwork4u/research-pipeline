# 一句話結論

States are freezing data-center grid interconnections over "ghost demand," but this looks like a self-correcting administrative bottleneck, not a proven structural one.

# Executive Summary

Texas Governor Abbott froze all new data-center grid interconnections on August 3, 2026, after requests reached roughly 474 gigawatts -- over five times peak demand -- with Pennsylvania and Ohio imposing comparable queue-verification controls; a Reuters review found over 700 gigawatts of similar requests nationally, more than ten times actual U.S. data-center power use. Regulators attribute most of this gap to duplicative, unfunded "ghost demand" filings rather than real demand, making this an administrative queue-integrity problem distinct from the physical equipment shortages and political-opposition moratoria this pipeline covered in August 2026. The freeze is real -- up to roughly 1,800 Texas projects are reported halted -- but ERCOT targets audit completion around December 2026, Pennsylvania explicitly favors self-generating projects, and Vistra's dedicated nuclear supply deal with Amazon shows well-capitalized developers can route around the general queue. That combination of a concrete resolution timeline and real substitutability keeps this a Growth Area friction rather than a proven Structural Bottleneck.

# Research Question / Why Now

**Primary question:** see front matter above.

**Sub-questions:**
- How large is the gap between headline interconnection-queue gigawatts and verified, financed demand, and what does that reveal about the reliability of the 474GW/700GW figures?
- What are ERCOT's and Pennsylvania's actual audit and verification timelines, and when might new interconnections resume at pre-freeze pace?
- Are major hyperscalers responding to the freeze by rerouting capital, or by pursuing behind-the-meter/dedicated-generation deals like Vistra's Comanche Peak arrangement?
- Is this a genuinely new bottleneck mechanism, or an acceleration of the political-opposition and curtailment-priority dynamics already identified in this pipeline's August 2026 reports?
- Does the strict Structural Bottleneck test hold for state-level interconnection-queue gatekeeping, or is this a temporary Growth-Area friction?

**Why now:** Texas's Governor Abbott directed a statewide interconnection audit on August 3, 2026 after requests hit ~474GW; Pennsylvania signed a comparable executive order August 18; a Reuters review published within the past week quantified the phenomenon nationally at over 700GW -- a concrete, dated, cross-state regulatory event distinct from anything already on this pipeline's watchlist.

**Thesis:** The 474GW/700GW figures are reported to be dominated by speculative, duplicative filings rather than financed demand, making queue-verification capacity -- not physical equipment -- the near-term constraint; but a concrete multi-quarter audit timeline and demonstrated substitutability via dedicated-supply deals mean this functions as a temporary friction rather than a durable structural bottleneck.

# Known Facts

- Governor Abbott's August 3, 2026 letter directed PUCT/ERCOT to audit all data centers in the interconnection process; non-compliant projects "must be denied connection," and no further data center may advance until the audit completes. _(evidence: ev-1, ev-6, ev-10)_
- Abbott's letter cited ~474GW of ERCOT interconnection requests (~90% data centers), over five times peak demand and up from ~48GW in 2023. _(evidence: ev-1, ev-3, ev-4)_
- ERCOT paused its "Batch Zero" large-load classification process in response and targets audit completion around December 2026. _(evidence: ev-10, ev-5, ev-13)_
- A Reuters review found large-load electricity requests exceeding 700GW nationally, over ten times actual U.S. data-center consumption; ten major non-Texas utilities logged ~270GW. _(evidence: ev-3)_
- Regulators attribute much of the gap to "ghost demand": duplicative, unfunded speculative filings; some utilities saw reported demand fall sharply once financial guardrails were imposed. _(evidence: ev-3, ev-11)_
- AEP Ohio's data-center tariff (load-study fees of $10,000-$100,000) is reported to have cut its data-center pipeline by more than half. _(evidence: ev-11)_
- Pennsylvania's Executive Order 2026-05 (August 18, 2026) applies stricter rules to data centers above 25MW, with clean-firm-energy requirements rising from 10% (2027) to 32% (2035), favoring self-generating projects. _(evidence: ev-2, ev-9, ev-14)_
- 43+ companies including Meta, OpenAI, Microsoft and AWS publicly supported Abbott's audit; up to ~1,800 Texas projects are reported halted pending it. _(evidence: ev-7, ev-12)_
- Vistra publicly supports the Texas pause and expects its 1.2GW Comanche Peak nuclear deal with Amazon to be unaffected. _(evidence: ev-8)_
- ERCOT's Year 1-6 MW allocation study is due April 9, 2027, with a final transmission plan expected fall 2027; data-center demand growth is estimated to have driven a $29.4 billion PJM capacity-cost increase over ~4 auctions. _(evidence: ev-13, ev-3)_

# Reasonable Inferences

- Because the headline figures are reported dominated by speculative filings, the effective near-term constraint is administrative verification capacity, not physical grid capacity -- a distinct mechanism from prior equipment-shortage and opposition bottlenecks. _(evidence: ev-3, ev-11)_
- Because Vistra's dedicated Comanche Peak deal and Pennsylvania's bring-your-own-generation preference both survive the freezes, large developers can substitute bespoke supply deals for the public queue -- evidence against "Hard" substitutability. _(evidence: ev-8, ev-9)_
- Because ERCOT has set a rough December 2026 audit target and a concrete April-fall 2027 allocation timeline, the freeze looks like a multi-quarter pause rather than an open-ended constraint, though timelines could slip given the audit's acknowledged lack of specifics as of mid-August. _(evidence: ev-5, ev-13, ev-7)_
- Because AEP Ohio's tariff already cut its pipeline by more than half, ghost-demand problems appear to resolve faster once financial guardrails are imposed than physical bottlenecks requiring years of lead time. _(evidence: ev-11)_
- Because 43 major hyperscalers endorsed rather than opposed the audit, incumbent large players may see queue cleanup as removing weaker speculative competitors rather than constraining themselves. _(evidence: ev-7)_

# Core Mechanism

States gate new data-center power access through interconnection queues that regulators must verify before granting connection. Since 2023, Texas's queue grew roughly tenfold to 474GW -- nationally, over 700GW -- because developers file speculative, often duplicative requests cheaply to secure position without committed financing. Once that gap became visible, Texas, Pennsylvania and Ohio each moved to freeze or gate new interconnections pending verification, directly slowing the pipeline of new AI data-center power access. But the mechanism differs from a physical shortage: AEP Ohio's tariff already cut its pipeline by more than half once fees were imposed, ERCOT has set concrete 2026-2027 target dates for resuming systematic processing, and well-capitalized developers such as Amazon (via Vistra's dedicated Comanche Peak nuclear supply) can bypass the public queue entirely through bespoke arrangements. The evidence supports "verification capacity is a real, near-term constraint that is actively being cleared" more than "a durable structural bottleneck on AI power access."

- Demand for interconnection is extreme (474GW Texas, 700GW national) and verification throughput is genuinely limited near-term, but substitutability is only Medium: bespoke behind-the-meter deals (Vistra-Amazon) and Pennsylvania's bring-your-own-generation preference let large developers route around the general queue.
- Combined with a concrete multi-quarter timeline to audit completion (~December 2026) and resumed systematic allocation (April-fall 2027), the evidence falls short of the strict test and is better classified a Growth Area friction that slows, but does not durably block, new AI power access.

# Final Assessment

**Verdict: VALIDATE** (confidence: medium)

Texas, Pennsylvania and Ohio have each moved in August 2026 to freeze or gate new data-center grid interconnections over "ghost demand" -- a genuinely new bottleneck mechanism, distinct from the physical equipment shortages and political-opposition moratoria this pipeline has covered. The freeze is real and immediate, with up to 1,800 Texas projects reported halted. But the evidence -- a concrete multi-quarter audit and allocation timeline, AEP Ohio's already-demonstrated pipeline cleanup, and Vistra's dedicated-supply workaround for Amazon -- does not clear the strict Structural Bottleneck bar, since substitutability is Medium rather than Hard and elasticity should improve once verification processes complete. This is a real near-term friction worth continued validation rather than a confirmed durable constraint.
