# One-Sentence Conclusion

Gas turbine manufacturing is a genuine structural bottleneck on AI power, though GE Vernova's own CEO says permitting and EPC timelines often bind first.

# Executive Summary

All four major heavy-duty gas turbine makers -- GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, Baker Hughes -- reported record, AI-data-center-cited order backlogs within a six-week window in mid-2026. GE Vernova's combined backlog and slot reservations hit 116 GW; Siemens Energy's firm backlog reached 69 GW with lead times of three-plus years; Mitsubishi's large-frame backlog reached 35 GW; Baker Hughes posted a record $37.1 billion industrial backlog. Wood Mackenzie estimates global manufacturing capacity at only 60-70 GW/year against roughly 110 GW of orders, projecting turbine prices to rise 195% by 2027. Behind-the-meter fuel cells (Bloom Energy) are scaling fast but remain roughly two orders of magnitude smaller than the OEMs' combined backlog. GE Vernova's own CEO argues turbines are frequently "not the gating item" once permitting and construction timelines are considered -- a genuine counterpoint even as turbine scarcity itself is real and priced into the market.

# Research Question / Why Now

**Primary question:** see front matter above.

**Sub-questions:** How much of the ~250 GW combined OEM order book is firm backlog versus non-firm slot reservations? Is capacity expansion (GE Vernova toward 30 GW/yr by 2030, Siemens toward 80 units/yr) closing Wood Mackenzie's 40-50 GW/year gap? Do fuel cells offer real substitutability at gigawatt scale? What does "not the gating item" mean for the bottleneck classification? What does developer behavior (reservations, prepayments) reveal about pricing power?

**Why now:** Within six weeks in mid-2026, GE Vernova (July 22), Baker Hughes (July 26), Siemens Energy (Aug. 5) and Mitsubishi Heavy Industries (early Aug.) all reported record, data-center-cited gas turbine order backlogs -- a first simultaneous disclosure this pipeline had not yet examined as a distinct node from grid transmission equipment (covered in the Aug. 23 transformer report).

**Thesis:** Gas turbine manufacturing meets the strict Structural Bottleneck test at the OEM layer, but the practical constraint on individual projects may already be shifting downstream to permitting and construction.

- Because all four OEMs hit records in the same six-week window citing data centers independently, this is an industry-wide capacity constraint, not one firm's execution issue. _(ev-1, ev-5, ev-7, ev-13)_
- Because Bloom Energy's total annual capacity (1-2 GW) is smaller than GE Vernova's single-quarter order growth (20 GW), fuel cells can bridge specific projects but not substitute at aggregate scale. _(ev-2, ev-12)_

# Core Mechanism

Heavy-duty gas turbines are the fastest dispatchable, at-scale power source data-center developers can add without waiting years for grid interconnection -- but only four companies worldwide (GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, Baker Hughes) manufacture them at scale, and each turbine requires specialized forging, casting and assembly capacity that takes years to build. AI-driven demand has stacked on top of pre-existing LNG and grid-reliability demand, pushing all four OEMs to record backlogs simultaneously and lead times to 3-6 years even as each expands production.

Behind-the-meter alternatives exist -- Bloom Energy's fuel cells deploy in 90-120 days -- but their combined annual manufacturing capacity (1-2 GW) is roughly two orders of magnitude smaller than the OEMs' combined backlog, so they can bridge individual projects, not substitute for turbines at the gigawatt scale a data-center campus needs. GE Vernova's own CEO complicates the pure-bottleneck narrative, arguing that permitting, EPC construction and fuel availability are often the actual pacing constraint across a project's roughly three-year timeline -- meaning turbine scarcity is real and priced into the market (a 195% price rise since 2019) even as it may not always be the binding constraint on any single project's schedule.

# Final Assessment

**Verdict: VALIDATE** (confidence: medium)

Heavy-duty gas turbine manufacturing meets the strict Structural Bottleneck test: extreme, simultaneously-confirmed demand across all four global OEMs; low supply elasticity given 3-6 year lead times against a 60-70 GW/year capacity ceiling; and hard substitutability, since the fastest-scaling alternative (fuel cells) remains roughly two orders of magnitude smaller than the OEMs' combined backlog. The genuine complication is GE Vernova's own CEO arguing turbines are often not the pacing constraint once permitting and construction are considered -- worth continued validation on whether the binding constraint is shifting downstream even as turbine scarcity itself persists and is increasingly priced into the market.
