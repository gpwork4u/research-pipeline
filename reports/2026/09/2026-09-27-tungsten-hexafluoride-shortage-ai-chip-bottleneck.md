# One-Sentence Conclusion

Japan's shutdown removed a quarter of global WF6 supply chipmaking cannot do without, but China's unrestricted WF6 exports may be quietly filling the gap.

# Executive Summary

China's January 2026 tightening of tungsten dual-use export rules cut high-purity tungsten-powder exports to Japan to zero, forcing Kanto Denka Kogyo and Central Glass -- roughly a quarter of global WF6 capacity -- to notify Samsung, SK Hynix and TSMC of a permanent halt from July 1, 2026. WF6 prices surged over 200% in China; Korean suppliers SK Specialty and Foosung notified 70-90% H2 2026 price hikes. Yet SK Hynix told investors in April it had diversified suppliers and secured enough inventory to avoid production impact, and China's own producers -- led by Peric Special Gases, up over 730% in H1 2026 -- are exempt from the controls and reportedly expanding. The bottleneck is real for the ex-China producer base; whether it binds AI chip output depends on how fast Chinese exports and buyer inventories absorb the shock.

# Research Question / Why Now

**Primary question:** see front matter above.

**Sub-questions:** How large is the Japanese shutdown relative to global WF6 supply, and how fast can Korean or Chinese producers fill it? Do Samsung, SK Hynix and TSMC's own statements show a material impact? Why is finished WF6 itself exempt from China's export controls even as upstream tungsten is restricted? How does this differ from the helium and bromine/HBr gas bottlenecks already assessed? Can non-China WF6 capacity realistically come online before 2027?

**Why now:** the Japanese shutdown took effect July 1, 2026; Peric's record H1 2026 rally and Korean suppliers' H2 2026 price hikes make this an active, unresolved shock as of September 2026 -- distinct from the helium (2026-08-27) and bromine/HBr (2026-09-11) bottlenecks already covered.

**Thesis:** see Core Mechanism below.

# Known Facts

- China's MOFCOM/GACC issued Announcement No. 91 on Dec 31, 2025, tightening tungsten dual-use export licensing effective Jan 1, 2026. _(s2)_
- China's high-purity tungsten-powder exports to Japan fell to effectively zero, Feb-Apr 2026. _(s4, s10, s8)_
- Kanto Denka Kogyo and Central Glass notified Samsung, SK Hynix and TSMC in April 2026 of a permanent WF6 halt from July 1, 2026 (last shipment June 30). _(s5, s12)_
- The two Japanese producers represent roughly 2,000-2,200 tonnes/year, about a quarter of global WF6 capacity. _(s5, s12, s13)_
- Tungsten powder feedstock is 60-70% of WF6 production cost, and Japan sources it almost entirely from China. _(s12)_
- WF6 (5N-grade) prices rose over 200% year-on-year by mid-2026. _(s3)_
- SK Specialty and Foosung notified Samsung, SK Hynix and DB HiTek of 70-90% WF6 price increases for H2 2026. _(s9)_
- SK Hynix's Q1 FY2026 earnings call (Apr 23, 2026): suppliers for helium, bromine and tungsten already diversified, sufficient inventory built, "no impact on our production." _(s1)_
- China's export controls restrict upstream tungsten materials (ore, APT, tungsten oxide) but not finished WF6 gas itself. _(s8)_
- Peric Special Gases (~2,200 t/yr WF6 capacity) was China's best-performing A-share in H1 2026, up over 730%; it reports >95% domestic and >30% international WF6 market coverage. _(s8, s7)_
- Kanto Denka Kogyo reported FY revenue of ~JPY 65.40bn (+4.89%) and earnings of ~JPY 3.79bn (+16.53%), a period before the shutdown's impact. _(s11)_
- WF6 deposits tungsten films via CVD in sub-7nm logic, 3D NAND and HBM, where rising layer counts raise per-wafer WF6 use. _(s3, s6)_
- Peric was reported expanding capacity in Sept 2026; China Shipbuilding Special Gas's H1 2026 revenue rose 83% YoY with WF6-linked income up nearly 3x. _(s6)_

# Reasonable Inferences

- China's controls target upstream tungsten but exempt finished WF6, likely shifting profit capture toward Chinese producers (Peric, China Shipbuilding Special Gas) and away from Japan's shuttered plants rather than removing global WF6 supply outright. _(s8, s6, s7)_
- SK Hynix's April 2026 "no impact" statement predates the July 1 shutdown and Korean suppliers' own H2 2026 price hikes, so it may understate the risk that has since emerged. _(s1, s9)_
- This mirrors the rare-earth and CXMT-memory pattern already seen in this pipeline: a China-controlled input chokepoint that simultaneously builds Chinese downstream market share. _(s8, s2)_
- Price hikes without reported output cuts suggest the near-term effect is a margin/cost bottleneck for chipmakers rather than an outright volume constraint so far. _(s9, s3, s1)_

# Core Mechanism

**Thesis:** China's tightened tungsten export controls forced the permanent shutdown of roughly a quarter of global WF6 capacity just as AI-driven HBM and advanced-logic demand for tungsten-fill CVD is rising -- but because finished WF6 itself is not export-controlled, Chinese producers can still export it, turning a shock centered outside China into a bottleneck that binds the ex-China producer base while being partly arbitraged away by Chinese exports, buyer inventory and Korean capacity.

Tungsten hexafluoride is the sole commercially viable precursor for the tungsten fill that connects transistor layers in advanced logic, 3D NAND and HBM; no drop-in substitute exists today. When China's tightened catalogue cut off Japan's tungsten-powder feedstock, Kanto Denka Kogyo and Central Glass had no way to keep producing and shut down permanently -- unlike Korean suppliers, who kept feedstock access and instead raised prices 70-90%. That combination (extreme demand, low ex-China elasticity, hard substitutability) is a textbook Structural Bottleneck for the ex-China producer base.

But the same rule that choked Japan's feedstock left the finished-WF6 export lane open, and Chinese producers -- who never lost access to Chinese tungsten -- are capturing the resulting price umbrella and expanding output. That is why SK Hynix could tell investors in April it faced no production impact: buyers can still source WF6, increasingly from China, at a higher price. Whether that substitution scales fast enough is the open question.

# Strongest Bear Case

- **Supply**: China's own WF6 producers have unrestricted feedstock and unrestricted finished-gas exports, and are reportedly expanding -- this could be a redistribution of market share, not a lasting global shortage.
- **Economics**: SK Hynix said in April it had diversified suppliers and secured sufficient inventory with no production impact.
- **Regulation**: China has eased controls on other critical minerals before; a similar reversal on tungsten would remove the root cause.
- **Competition**: Korean suppliers are raising prices rather than cutting volumes, so the near-term effect looks like margin pressure, not a stoppage.
- **Technology**: Alternative tungsten-fill chemistries could reduce WF6 intensity per wafer over a multi-year horizon, though none was identified as imminent.

# Final Assessment

**Verdict: VALIDATE** (confidence: medium)

The ex-China WF6 producer base and its upstream tungsten feedstock both meet the strict structural-bottleneck test: extreme AI-linked demand, low near-term elasticity evidenced by a permanent Japanese shutdown with no announced replacement, and hard substitutability because WF6 has no viable alternative for tungsten-fill CVD. But China's own WF6 industry -- exempt from the export controls that caused the shock, and reportedly expanding -- fails that same elasticity test, and SK Hynix's own April 2026 statement of no production impact shows buyers may be successfully hedging so far. This is VALIDATE, not BUILD NOW or PAUSE: whether Chinese exports and buyer inventories absorb the shock before it reaches AI chip output should become clear in the Q3 2026 and Q4 2026 earnings cycle.
