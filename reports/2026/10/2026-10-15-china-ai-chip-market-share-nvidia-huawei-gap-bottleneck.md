# 一句話結論

IDC's 34%-to-28% Nvidia-Huawei split shows real but moderate movement, undercutting both the "zero share" and "90%-domestic" claims.

# Executive Summary

IDC data reported October 13-14, 2026 show Nvidia held 34% of China's AI-accelerator shipments in H1 2026 (678,000 units, -44% YoY) versus Huawei's 28% (572,000 units), narrowing their gap from 31 percentage points a year earlier to 6. That 34% share sits far above Jensen Huang's "dropped to zero" framing of recognized China revenue, and domestic vendors' combined ~47% share is well short of the ~90% some analysts projected for end-2026. This pipeline's September 4, 2026 report identified US remote-access enforcement authority -- not chip supply -- as the binding Structural Bottleneck; the Senate bill addressing it remains stalled roughly ten months in committee, and BIS's own drafted rule is reportedly shelved. The new data reaffirms, rather than resolves, that finding.

# Research Question / Why Now

**Primary question:** see front matter above.

**Sub-questions:**
- What exactly does IDC's H1 2026 China AI-accelerator shipment data show for Nvidia, Huawei, and other domestic vendors, versus the full-year 2025 baseline?
- Does a 34% Nvidia share, with GPU shipments down 26% YoY, support or contradict the "fell to zero" / "largely conceded" characterizations circulating earlier in 2026?
- Has the Senate's Remote Access Security Act (S.3519) or Commerce's drafted remote-access rule advanced, stalled further, or been abandoned since September?
- How does Nvidia's own public positioning square with the IDC data?
- Does domestic vendors' combined ~47% share represent material progress toward the ~90% end-2026 projection, or a slower pace?

**Why now:** IDC's H1 2026 China AI-accelerator vendor-share data, reported October 13-14, 2026, directly fulfills the open watchlist validation event from this pipeline's September 4, 2026 report: "Updated analyst tracking ... of Huawei/domestic Chinese AI chip market share."

**Thesis:** The IDC data shows real but moderate domestic-substitution progress -- far short of either side's strongest claims -- leaving the US enforcement-authority bottleneck this pipeline identified in September still open and still binding.

# Known Facts

- IDC data (Oct 13-14, 2026) put China's H1 2026 AI-accelerator shipments at 2.008 million units; Nvidia shipped 678,000 (34% share, GPU shipments overall -26% YoY to 991,000 units); Huawei shipped 572,000 (28%); Alibaba's T-Head held 7%, Cambricon 5%, Baidu's Kunlunxin 4%, Hygon 3%. _(s1, s2, s3)_
- The Nvidia-Huawei gap narrowed from 31 percentage points a year earlier to 6 points in H1 2026. _(s1)_
- A separate April 2026 Reuters/IDC report (full-year 2025) put Nvidia's share at ~55% (~2.2M cards), domestic vendors at ~41% combined, with Huawei leading domestic vendors at ~812,000 units. _(s4)_
- Nvidia's Q2 FY2027 guidance (ended July 26, 2026) assumed zero recognized China data-center revenue, and Jensen Huang has said Nvidia's China share "dropped to zero" -- a framing that does not on its face match IDC's 34% shipment-share tracking. _(s10, s1)_
- Huang has repeatedly called Huawei "formidable," and Nvidia's most recent annual SEC filing lists Huawei among named competitors for a second consecutive year, across chip, cloud, compute and networking categories. _(s6, s7)_
- Senate bill S.3519, introduced Dec. 17, 2025, shows no recorded committee or floor action as of October 2026 -- ten months after introduction and nine months after the House passed its companion bill 369-22. _(s8)_
- A BIS final rule effective Jan. 15, 2026 moved H200/MI325X China/Macau export review to case-by-case, conditioned on performance caps and US lab testing. _(s9)_
- Thinly-sourced, uncorroborated reporting states BIS has shelved its own drafted third-country GPU rental rule while compiling separate smuggling/remote-access country lists. _(s11)_
- Huawei targets ~600,000 Ascend 910C units and ~$12B AI chip revenue in 2026. _(s12)_

# Reasonable Inferences

- Because Nvidia's 34% tracked share far exceeds the "dropped to zero" framing and the most bullish ~8-10% end-2026 forecasts, real Chinese access to Nvidia compute -- via compliant chips, rental, or other channels -- is likely running higher than the strongest "controls are working" narratives suggested in September. _(s1, s4, s10)_
- Because the Senate bill remains stalled and BIS's rule is reportedly shelved, the enforcement-authority gap identified in September shows no sign of closing -- if accurate, Commerce may be stepping back rather than forward. _(s8, s11)_
- Because domestic share rose only from ~41% to ~47% rather than toward ~90%, the substitution race is progressing more slowly than the most aggressive forecasts implied, keeping the enforcement gap's stakes elevated. _(s1, s4)_
- Nvidia's continued "formidable"-competitor framing, despite guidance excluding China data-center revenue, suggests Nvidia itself treats the contest as unresolved. _(s6, s7, s10)_

# Core Mechanism

US export controls restrict advanced Nvidia chip sales to China, but IDC's H1 2026 tracking shows Nvidia still shipping 678,000 units for a 34% share -- a volume inconsistent with "zero" framing of the market. That gap is explained by this pipeline's September thesis: a documented remote-access rental loophole, plus export-compliant lower-performance chips, still channel meaningful Nvidia compute into China alongside Huawei's rising but not yet dominant Ascend shipments. The Senate bill meant to close the loophole has sat in committee ten months, and BIS's own administrative fix is reported shelved, so the enforcement-authority bottleneck remains open on both the legislative and regulatory tracks while the market-share data it is meant to address keeps shifting gradually, not abruptly, toward domestic substitution.

# Final Assessment

**Verdict: VALIDATE** (confidence: medium)

IDC's H1 2026 data -- Nvidia at 34% share versus Huawei at 28%, a gap narrowed from 31 to 6 points -- shows genuine domestic-substitution progress, but far more moderate than either the "Nvidia conceded" or "90%-domestic" claims this pipeline flagged in September. A 34% Nvidia share implies meaningful ongoing Chinese access to Nvidia compute through compliant chips and/or the remote-access channels this pipeline identified as exploiting a real enforcement gap. With the Senate bill stalled ten months and BIS's own rule reportedly shelved, that enforcement-authority bottleneck remains open, not resolved, warranting continued validation rather than a verdict change.
