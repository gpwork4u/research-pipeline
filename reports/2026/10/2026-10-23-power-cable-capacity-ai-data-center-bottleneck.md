# 一句話結論

Nexans' CFO says cable capacity is now the AI data-center bottleneck, and the company's own order data -- double-digit growth, three-year line lead times -- backs him up.

# Executive Summary

On October 22, 2026, Reuters reported Nexans CFO Vincent Piquet saying cable production capacity is becoming a bottleneck for AI-driven data-center construction, since a new XLPE production line takes roughly three years to build. The same day, Nexans' own nine-month 2026 results showed its data-center-linked PWR-Connect and PWR-Grid segments growing 10.5% and 14.8% organically in Q3, while its long-haul PWR-Transmission segment actually declined 1.4%. Two days earlier, ABB reported record Q3 2026 orders of $12.06 billion (+40% YoY), with "triple-digit" growth in data centers -- yet ABB's own CEO said ABB itself is "normally not the limiting factor" on lead time. The evidence supports a real but narrow bottleneck concentrated in medium-voltage/data-center-connection cable manufacturing, not electrification equipment or long-haul transmission cable broadly.

# Research Question / Why Now

**Primary question:** see front matter above.

**Thesis:** Data-center-linked cable demand is outrunning the ~3-year lead time to add XLPE production capacity, but the strain is concentrated, not universal.

# Reasonable Inferences

- Demand for data-center-connection cable plausibly outruns the ~3-year capacity-build timeline -- a distinct node from the transformer/copper bottlenecks already documented. _(s1, s2)_

- ABB's own "not the limiting factor" statement suggests electrification equipment has better supply elasticity than cable manufacturing specifically. _(s5, s7)_

# Core Mechanism

Hyperscale AI data centers require large volumes of medium-voltage cable to connect substations, switchgear and IT load, and that cable is made on dedicated XLPE "CV" production lines that take roughly three years to build from scratch. When demand for this specific product category accelerates faster than new lines can be commissioned, suppliers like Nexans gain pricing power and can be more selective about allocation -- exactly the dynamic its CFO described, and exactly why customers like Enedis now sign six-to-seven-year contracts rather than compete annually for spot capacity.

This mechanism is narrower than it first appears. Nexans' own results show it does not apply evenly: PWR-Transmission, the long-haul/subsea cable business, is decelerating, and ABB -- which supplies adjacent electrification equipment riding the same AI data-center demand wave -- says it is not itself capacity-constrained. The bottleneck is therefore plausibly a specific-product-category phenomenon (medium-voltage/connection cable) rather than a blanket AI-power-equipment shortage.

# Strongest Bear Case

- **Economics**: ABB's own CEO says ABB is "normally not the limiting factor" on lead time -- undercutting a blanket electrification-shortage narrative.
- **Demand**: Nexans' own long-haul PWR-Transmission segment is decelerating, not accelerating, showing the strain is not uniform across cable types.
- **Supply**: A 39% PWR-Grid Accessories capacity increase by 2028 is already funded and underway -- faster than the 3-year full-line benchmark.
- **Execution**: Heavy reliance on multi-year frame agreements could leave new capacity stranded if AI capex decelerates before it completes.
- **Competition**: Several credible global cable makers can add capacity, unlike single-source bottlenecks such as EUV lithography or HBM.

# Final Assessment

**Verdict: VALIDATE** (confidence: medium)

The evidence -- a direct CFO statement, same-day earnings confirming double-digit growth in the exact product category named, and a three-year capacity-build constraint -- is strong enough to track as a genuine new node, but it is narrower than "cable is short." The long-haul transmission segment is decelerating and ABB explicitly denies being capacity-limited, so this bottleneck should be tracked specifically as medium-voltage/data-center-connection cable, not generalized across all power-delivery equipment. The next two quarters of earnings from Nexans, ABB and Prysmian should clarify whether this is durable or a temporary backlog.
