# One-Sentence Conclusion

TSMC reiterated, not raised, its 2026 capex despite record results -- evidence packaging is easing toward a Growth Area while wafer capacity stays the real Structural Bottleneck.

# Executive Summary

TSMC's October 15, 2026 Q3 call delivered record revenue of $46.7 billion (+16.2% QoQ) and Q4 guidance implying roughly 58% YoY growth, yet management only reiterated -- not raised again -- its $60-64 billion 2026 capex range. CoWoS/packaging commentary was flat versus July ("gap remains large," never "sold out" or "fully booked"), while OSAT and Intel second-sourcing keep expanding. Meanwhile the 2-nanometer ramp alone cost 1.3 points of gross margin, and management called further capacity growth "too early to quantify." This reaffirms this pipeline's September-October downgrade of packaging to a Growth Area while leaving leading-edge wafer capacity as the binding Structural Bottleneck. The main uncertainty: TSMC discloses neither CoWoS capacity nor lead times, so the "gap narrowing" claim rests on analyst estimates that disagree by nearly 50%.

**Thesis:** Packaging eases toward Growth Area; wafer capacity remains the deeper Structural Bottleneck.

# Known Facts

- Q3 2026: revenue NT$1,494.24B (~$46.7B, above July's $44.6-45.8B guide), net income NT$763.06B (+68.7% YoY), EPS NT$29.43, revenue +16.2% QoQ. Gross margin 66.4% (-1.3pp QoQ), operating margin 58.7%, net margin 51.1%. _(s1, s3, s4)_
- Margin decline attributed mainly to N2 ramp dilution. Node mix: N3 33%, N5 28%, N2 8%; nodes 7nm-and-below totaled 79% of wafer revenue. _(s1, s4)_
- Q4 2026 guidance: revenue $52.3-54.3B (~58% YoY implied), gross margin 65-67%. _(s1, s9)_
- FY2026 capex guidance reiterated at $60-64B (unchanged since the July raise from $52-56B); management said 2026-2028 capex will be significantly higher than the prior three years, with no specific 2027 number given. _(s2, s4, s5)_
- On CoWoS, Wei said it remains customers' leading choice, the demand-supply gap "remains large," capacity is rising "as fast as possible" but growth is "too early to quantify"; TSMC is working with GlobalFoundries on backend capacity and developing larger reticle sizes. _(s2, s8)_
- A third-party review found TSMC has never used "sold out" or "fully booked" language on any 2026 call; TSMC does not publish CoWoS capacity or lead times. _(s8)_
- TSMC beat consensus (EPS ~$4.60 vs. $4.42) yet shares fell, which coverage linked to broader AI-capex-sustainability scrutiny. _(s3, s4)_
- Analysts (TrendForce) estimate the CoWoS gap narrowing from ~20% to ~10% by end-2026 on a ~10x build-out since 2023; capacity estimates for end-2026 range widely, from ~95,000 (JPMorgan) to 120,000-140,000 wafers/month (TrendForce). _(s6, s10)_
- Second-sourcing is emerging but small: OSAT partners (UMC, Amkor) estimated at ~50,000-60,000 wafers/month; Intel's EMIB-T estimated at ~15,000-20,000 wafers/month in 2027, rising to ~40,000-45,000 in 2028 -- all analyst estimates, not company guidance. _(s6, s7)_

# Reasonable Inferences

- Reiterating rather than raising capex despite a beat suggests TSMC is already spending near its judged-optimal pace -- more consistent with wafer capacity, not packaging, being the tighter near-term constraint. _(s2, s4, s5)_
- Packaging language is essentially unchanged in substance since July rather than escalating, and "sold out" has never been used -- consistent with the prior Growth Area downgrade holding or deepening. _(s2, s8)_
- The N2 margin hit, combined with no comparable wafer second-source (unlike packaging's emerging alternatives), points to wafer fabrication as the node still meeting the strict Structural Bottleneck test. _(s1, s4, s6)_
- Shares falling despite a beat-and-raise suggests the market may be pricing AI-capex sustainability risk into TSMC specifically. _(s3, s4)_

# Unknowns

- TSMC's actual CoWoS capacity and lead times are undisclosed; end-2026 analyst estimates span ~95,000-140,000 wafers/month, too wide to independently verify the "gap narrowing to ~10%" claim.
- Whether 2027 capex lands near analyst estimates ($75B-$105B cited across sources) or diverges materially; TSMC gave no 2027 figure this quarter.
- Whether Intel's EMIB-T or OSAT spillover will scale on the estimated timelines; both are unproven at volume.
- Whether the post-earnings share decline is durable or short-lived; no multi-day price data was available.

# Core Mechanism

TSMC's own capital-allocation behavior is the clearest signal here: a quarter that beat guidance and raised the Q4 outlook to roughly 58% YoY growth would, under a true packaging-only bottleneck, likely prompt another packaging-specific capex increase. Instead, capex was merely reiterated at $60-64B, and the pain point visible in the numbers -- a 1.3-point margin hit -- came from the N2 wafer ramp, not packaging. Combined with management's packaging language holding steady since July (never escalating to "sold out") and real, if small, second-sourcing emerging (OSAT spillover, Intel's EMIB-T), the balance of evidence points to packaging easing into a Growth Area while leading-edge wafer fabrication -- where no comparable alternative supplier exists -- remains the binding Structural Bottleneck this pipeline has tracked since August.

# Final Assessment

**Verdict: VALIDATE** (confidence: medium)

TSMC's October 15 call is best read as confirmation, not reversal, of this pipeline's standing split: packaging continues easing toward a Growth Area (flat demand-supply language, never "sold out," real if small second-sourcing), while leading-edge wafer capacity -- where capex was only reiterated despite record results and a margin-costly N2 ramp -- remains the Structural Bottleneck.
