# One-Sentence Conclusion

A named Samsung executive has, for the first time, put an on-record percentage on the DRAM wafer-capacity shift this pipeline previously only inferred from downstream effects.

# Executive Summary

TrendForce's September 29, 2026 report quoted Samsung Device Solutions executive Kim Taewoo stating HBM will take roughly 30% of industry DRAM wafer capacity in 2027, up from about 20% today -- the first company-sourced figure to quantify the capacity-reallocation mechanism this pipeline previously only inferred from downstream data in its September and October 2026 reports. TrendForce's own model shows HBM's wafer-input share (18% to 22% to 30% across 2025-2027) rising faster than its bit-supply share (8% to 9% to 13%), meaning each unit of HBM output removes disproportionate wafer capacity from the shared pool. This lands alongside Samsung's record preliminary Q3 2026 results (~107.4 trillion won operating profit, +782.5% YoY) and forecasts from TrendForce and KB Securities of 100-121% HBM price growth in 2027. Yet Samsung's own April 2026 call conceded conventional DRAM is currently more profitable than HBM, and management said it will keep a balanced mix rather than pivot toward conventional DRAM for near-term gain -- the supplier best positioned to relieve the shortage has said it will not.

# Core Mechanism

This pipeline's September and October 2026 reports inferred that Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron were crowding out conventional DRAM by directing shared wafer capacity toward higher-margin HBM, evidenced only indirectly through downstream pricing, shipment and supplier-visibility data. TrendForce's September 29 report closes the gap on the supply side: a named Samsung executive states the mechanism directly, putting HBM's share of industry DRAM wafer capacity at roughly 20% today, rising to roughly 30% in 2027. TrendForce's own model shows why this compounds: HBM's wafer-input share rises faster than its bit-supply share, so scaling HBM consumes wafer capacity disproportionately. Samsung's record Q3 2026 preliminary results confirm memory profitability remains exceptional, yet Samsung's April 2026 admission -- conventional DRAM is currently *more* profitable than HBM -- shows the company is not chasing near-term margin by rebalancing toward conventional output; it is holding a "balanced" mix while betting the profitability gap narrows through 2027 HBM price appreciation (forecast at 100-121%) rather than conventional-DRAM relief.

# Strongest Bear Case

- **Supply**: ~$870B in combined capex commitments could bring capacity online faster than late 2028, shifting wafer share back toward conventional DRAM sooner than forecast.
- **Economics**: If 100-121% 2027 HBM price growth materializes, rising HBM margins (not easing conventional-DRAM prices) could make further HBM expansion more attractive, undercutting the "stable allocation" framing.
- **Demand**: The 30% figure is a forecast, not a locked allocation; a hyperscaler capex deceleration would reduce pressure to reach it.
- **Execution**: The figure comes through secondary reprints of a Reuters interview, not a formal earnings-call disclosure, and could be imprecise or Samsung-specific rather than industry-wide.

# Final Assessment

**Verdict: VALIDATE** (confidence: medium)

A named Samsung executive's on-record 20%-to-30% wafer-share figure is the strongest supply-side confirmation yet of this pipeline's DRAM/HBM crowd-out thesis, reinforcing the Structural Bottleneck classification for shared wafer capacity. But the figure arrives through secondary reprints of a single interview, not a formal disclosure, and Samsung's own forecast that the profitability gap narrows via rising HBM prices (not falling conventional-DRAM prices) leaves real uncertainty about whether this is a stable multi-year allocation or one that will shift again with relative pricing -- validate against Samsung's full Q3 call and SK Hynix's upcoming results before treating 30% as settled.
