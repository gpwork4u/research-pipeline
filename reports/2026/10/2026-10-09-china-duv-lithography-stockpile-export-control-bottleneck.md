# 一句話結論

China's 343-tool DUV stockpile and a nascent homegrown lithography program erode the export-control bottleneck at 7nm, but low yields keep it from breaking it.

# Executive Summary

A September 2026 Center for Technology & Statecraft report estimates Chinese fabs hold 343 immersion DUV lithography systems -- about 270 of them ASML's exportable NXT:1980i -- worth over $13 billion, enough per the authors' estimate to support 100,000+ Ascend-910C-class chips per tool annually. China has also started producing homegrown immersion DUV tools, with Huawei backing a 12-unit 2026 target already under test at SMIC. Yet reported Ascend 910C yields remain near 20-40%, domestic tools still import lenses and light sources, and ASML's own China revenue share has fallen from roughly 36% to 14% in three quarters. The bottleneck is eroding at the 7nm-class margin, not broken at the true leading edge.

# Research Question / Why Now

**Primary question:** see front matter above.

**Sub-questions:**
- How large is China's confirmed DUV-immersion stockpile, and how did the NXT:1980i licensing gap form?
- How far has China's homegrown immersion-DUV program actually progressed, and what foreign inputs remain?
- What do Ascend 910C yield reports reveal about DUV multi-patterning as an EUV substitute?
- How is ASML's disclosed China revenue mix responding to the control regime?
- What is the status of the MATCH Act and Dutch licensing rules that could close remaining gaps?

**Why now:** The CTS report's 343-tool estimate was published late September 2026 and amplified across trade press through October 7-8, 2026, alongside confirmation that Huawei-backed domestic DUV tools are already under test at SMIC.

**Thesis:** China's DUV stockpile and emerging homegrown tool program let it approximate, at a real cost and yield penalty, 7nm-class AI chip output -- eroding the export-control bottleneck without replacing leading-edge capability.

# Known Facts

- A CTS report (late Sept. 2026) by Nicholas Brown and Saif Khan estimates ~343 immersion DUV systems at Chinese-owned fabs by early 2026, ~270 of them ASML's NXT:1980i. _(s1, s2)_
- The same report estimates over $13B spent on NXT:1980i tools in 2024-2025, and that one NXT:1980i can support 100,000+ Ascend-910C-equivalent chips/year even at sub-leading yields; it recommends a China-wide DUVi export ban. _(s1, s2)_
- The Netherlands required its own license for the NXT:2000i and newer from Sept. 2023, extending this to the NXT:1980i in Sept. 2024; case-by-case approval remains possible. _(s15)_
- Reuters (Jul. 28, 2026) reported China has begun producing homegrown immersion DUV tools via Shanghai Aishengna (founded 2023, incorporating Yuliangsheng/SMEE teams), targeting ~5 units in 2026 and ~20 in 2027, for SMIC, Hua Hong and CXMT. _(s10)_
- TrendForce (Sept. 8, 2026, citing the FT) reported Huawei backs a higher 12-unit 2026 target for Yuliangsheng's tool, already tested by SMIC and on Huawei's own lines, still dependent on imported lenses/light sources (Gigaphoton, Cymer). _(s11)_
- ASML's Q2 2026 call reported EUR9.3B net sales, EUR6.6B net system sales, EUR2.9B net income, and raised FY2026 guidance to EUR43-45B. _(s4)_
- Secondary reporting shows ASML's mainland China revenue share falling from ~36% (Q4 2025) to ~19% (Q1 2026) to ~14% (Q2 2026); ASML guided ~20% for full-year 2026. _(s5, s6, s7)_
- The MATCH Act (H.R. 8170), introduced Apr. 2, 2026, seeks China-wide restrictions on exporting/servicing chipmaking equipment including DUV; the House Foreign Affairs Committee advanced a narrowed version Apr. 22, 2026; no floor vote confirmed. _(s8, s9)_
- Reported Ascend 910C yield rose to ~40% (FT, via DIGITIMES Asia) from ~20% in Sept. 2024, with SMIC using DUV multi-patterning for 7nm-class output given no EUV access; target is 60%. _(s13)_
- Morgan Stanley reportedly cut SMIC's Huawei-linked AI chip revenue forecast over 50% on weak yields; other reporting cites a 600,000-unit 2026 Ascend 910C target. _(s14)_
- ASML CEO Christophe Fouquet downplayed near-term lithography rivals in May 2026, while separately warning that overly aggressive China restrictions could accelerate Chinese self-sufficiency. _(s12)_

# Reasonable Inferences

- Because the stockpile and domestic tools both target 7nm-class logic, they threaten the control regime's bite at Ascend's current node, not ASML's EUV-dependent sub-7nm leading edge, which stays unreachable to China. _(s1, s2, s13)_
- Because domestic DUV tools still import lenses and light sources, the workaround shifted the chokepoint from whole-tool licensing to a narrower component supply chain, not eliminated it. _(s11)_
- Persistently sub-50% reported yields even as output targets scale imply the workaround trades cost and yield for independence from EUV -- a partial substitute, not a replacement. _(s13, s14)_

# Core Mechanism

Export controls on EUV lithography aimed to deny China the tooling needed for leading-edge AI chips. China's response has been twofold: stockpile the one DUV-immersion model (NXT:1980i) still exportable under Dutch case-by-case licensing, and simultaneously fund a Huawei-backed domestic immersion-DUV program now under test at SMIC. Both paths use multi-patterning -- stacking more DUV exposure steps -- to approximate 7nm-class logic without EUV. This works, but imperfectly: reported Ascend 910C yields near 20-40% sit well below leading-edge norms, and the domestic tools still depend on imported lenses and light sources. The control regime's bite at 7nm is visibly eroding -- ASML's China revenue share has nearly halved in three quarters -- but the yield and import gaps show China is approximating, not replacing, leading-edge capability.

# Final Assessment

**Verdict: VALIDATE** (confidence: medium)

China's 343-tool DUV stockpile plus a nascent Huawei-backed homegrown immersion-DUV program give Chinese fabs a real, if costly, path to approximate 7nm-class AI chip output without EUV -- visibly eroding the export-control bottleneck, evidenced by ASML's China revenue share nearly halving in three quarters. But reported Ascend 910C yields of 20-40%, continued reliance on imported lenses and light sources, and unconfirmed domestic tool delivery targets show this is an approximation, not a replacement, of leading-edge capability. The true leading edge (sub-7nm, EUV-dependent) remains a Structural Bottleneck; China's workaround is a Growth Area worth continued monitoring. ASML's Q3 2026 earnings call and confirmed domestic tool deliveries are the next real tests.
