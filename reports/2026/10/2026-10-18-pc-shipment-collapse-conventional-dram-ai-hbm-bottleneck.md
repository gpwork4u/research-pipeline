# One-Sentence Conclusion

IDC's confirmed 20.1% YoY PC shipment collapse turns September's single-OEM DRAM crowd-out warning into an industry-wide fact, even as price-growth forecasts decelerate.

# Executive Summary

IDC's October 8, 2026 data show global PC shipments fell 20.1% YoY to 62.7 million units in Q3 2026 -- a second straight quarterly decline, attributed to inventory pulled forward ahead of memory price increases plus ongoing supply constraints. That is the first industry-wide confirmation of the downstream damage this pipeline's September 13 report anticipated from AI/HBM crowding out conventional DRAM. TrendForce's September 30 forecast still shows conventional DRAM prices rising in Q4 2026 (10-15% QoQ), but decelerating sharply from the roughly 58-63% QoQ pace reported earlier in 2026 -- an ambiguous signal. Micron's record fiscal Q4 2026 results (DRAM at 73% of revenue) came with management saying it has no visibility into supply-demand rebalancing and that new capacity won't arrive before late 2028. Forward indicators specific to the PC/module channel -- RDIMM bit-growth lagging server CPU growth, and a module-maker CEO warning of a possible 70%+ YoY cut to 2027 module allocations -- suggest the consumer-facing squeeze keeps tightening even as blended price growth slows. The antitrust case alleging coordinated restriction remains unresolved, with a motion-to-dismiss hearing reset to November 13, 2026.

# Known Facts

- Global PC shipments fell 20.1% YoY to 62.7M units in Q3 2026 -- a second straight decline, -9.1% QoQ. _(s3, s4)_
- IDC attributed this mainly to pulled-forward inventory plus ongoing supply constraints and elevated prices; outlook seen worsening before improving. _(s3, s4)_
- By vendor: Lenovo 14.9M, HP 10.3M, Dell 7.6M, Apple 5.9M, ASUS 5.5M units; Apple's share rose to ~9.5% from ~8.5%. _(s3, s4)_
- TrendForce (Sept 30): Q4 2026 conventional DRAM +10-15% QoQ, NAND +15-20% QoQ -- slower than earlier 2026 pace; suppliers still prioritize server capacity; PC DRAM supply may decline further in 2027. _(s5, s6)_
- Micron fiscal Q4 2026: revenue ~$54.2B (DRAM ~$39.8B/73%, NAND ~$14.1B/26%); FY2026 revenue ~$133.2B, +256% YoY. _(s1, s11)_
- Micron management: supply constraints persist through FY2027-2028; no visibility into rebalancing; 26 strategic agreements cover >35% of revenue through 2030; ~$32B customer deposits. _(s1, s11)_
- RDIMM bit growth (~15-20%/yr) lags server CPU growth; 2027 server DRAM shortfall "essentially confirmed"; Apacer CEO warns module-maker DRAM supply could fall 70%+ YoY in 2027. _(s9, s8)_
- Garciaguirre v. Samsung antitrust case: reply brief filed Oct 7, 2026; motion-to-dismiss hearing reset to Nov 13, 2026. _(s2, s10)_
- 32GB DDR5-6000 kit retail price ~$449.97 on Oct 14, 2026, up from ~$400 in August. _(s13)_
- Barclays (Oct 2026): DDR4 contract prices +~24% over three months, DDR5 +~20%; sources disagree whether shortage runs to 2028 or 2029. _(s7)_

# Core Mechanism

September's report found Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron directing shared DRAM wafer capacity toward higher-margin HBM, crowding out conventional DDR4/DDR5 supply, with Lenovo's cost disclosures as the clearest evidence so far. IDC's October 8 data moves that evidence from one OEM's margin pressure to an industry-wide volume collapse: global PC shipments fell a fifth year over year, a second consecutive decline IDC does not expect to reverse soon. The mechanism continues to run through Micron's own disclosures: record revenue is now 73% DRAM, management has no visibility into rebalancing, new capacity doesn't arrive before late 2028, and over a third of revenue through 2030 is already pre-committed to large strategic customers -- leaving the PC/module channel bidding for what's left. TrendForce's forecast complicates a simple "worsening indefinitely" story: price growth is decelerating sharply from its mid-2026 pace, even as the same forecast says PC DRAM supply could decline further in 2027. Forward indicators specific to the PC/module channel -- RDIMM bit-growth lagging server CPU demand, a module-maker's own supply-cut warning -- argue the physical squeeze on that channel keeps tightening regardless of the blended price-growth rate.

# Strongest Bear Case

- **Demand**: IDC attributes part of the Q3 drop to pulled-forward inventory, not pure supply-constrained demand destruction; shipments could stabilize once channels normalize.
- **Economics**: Price growth is decelerating sharply (10-15% QoQ vs. a reported ~58-63% pace earlier in 2026); continued deceleration could undercut the "worsening indefinitely" framing.
- **Supply**: Samsung/SK Hynix's ~$870B combined capacity commitment and Micron's rising FY2027 capex could shift some output back toward conventional DRAM faster than forecast.
- **Regulation**: The antitrust case could be dismissed on procedural grounds at the November 13 hearing without ever reaching the coordination allegations, leaving the question permanently unresolved.

# Final Assessment

**Verdict: VALIDATE** (confidence: medium)

IDC's October 8 data converts September's single-OEM DRAM crowd-out warning into an industry-wide fact: global PC shipments fell a fifth year over year, a second straight decline. Micron's record results and no-visibility commentary, plus PC/module-specific forward indicators, keep conventional DRAM classified as a Structural Bottleneck. But TrendForce's sharp price-growth deceleration and the unresolved antitrust case leave genuine ambiguity about whether the worst has passed or merely paused -- this should be validated against Q4 2026/Q1 2027 shipment and pricing data and the November 13 court hearing before any firmer classification.
