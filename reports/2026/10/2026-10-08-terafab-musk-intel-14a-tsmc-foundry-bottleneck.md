# 一句話結論

Musk kept TSMC out of Terafab on Oct 7, but a two-day stock swing on mere rumor shows the market still treats TSMC's involvement as decisive.

# Executive Summary

On October 7, 2026, Elon Musk stated his companies -- not TSMC -- will build and run Terafab, the vertically-integrated AI chip complex Tesla, SpaceX, xAI and Intel are developing in Grimes County, Texas, saying TSMC could at most "sublease part of the Terafab." This followed a two-day scare: Musk's October 4-5 confirmation of "discussions" with TSMC knocked Intel shares down 2-4% and lifted TSMC's, on fear Intel's unproven 14A node -- Terafab's only named manufacturing process -- would be displaced. Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan reaffirmed Intel's role the same day, and Intel shares recovered. SpaceX's own SEC filings describe Terafab as a non-binding "general framework" with no committed capex schedule. The episode is a genuine test of vertical integration against the TSMC-centered foundry bottleneck, but the market's sensitivity to rumor, the unproven 14A node, and the non-binding legal structure together argue the bottleneck remains Structural rather than resolved.

# Reasonable Inferences

- Because the market moved Intel's and TSMC's stock on a mere two-day rumor of TSMC joining Terafab, investors appear to price TSMC's direct participation -- not Intel's 14A node alone -- as the more credible signal of Terafab's manufacturing viability. _(s9, s5, s7)_
- Because SpaceX's own SEC filings describe Terafab as a non-binding "general framework" with no committed capex schedule, it functions today more as a strategic hedge and public-commitment device than a contracted manufacturing project. _(s1, s2, s13)_
- Because Terafab's capacity, even if built, is designed to serve only Tesla/SpaceX/xAI's own demand rather than the broader merchant market, its success would not by itself relieve the TSMC-centered bottleneck for other AI buyers. _(s4, s12, s1)_

# Core Mechanism

AI compute buyers that cannot secure enough TSMC-allocated leading-edge capacity have one structural option besides waiting: fund their own fab using an alternative process node. Terafab is that bet -- Tesla and SpaceX are underwriting Intel's unproven 14A node as a dedicated, vertically-integrated capacity source outside TSMC's allocation system. But the bet only pays off if 14A actually qualifies at volume on a competitive timeline, and SpaceX's own disclosures treat every deliverable as still undetermined. The market's reflexive two-day swing on a mere rumor of TSMC joining the project reveals that, absent TSMC's participation or a proven alternative node, investors do not yet believe vertical integration removes the underlying capacity constraint -- it merely relocates the bet to a single unproven supplier.

# Final Assessment

**Verdict: VALIDATE** (confidence: medium)

Musk's October 7, 2026 decision keeps Terafab a genuine, if fragile, vertical-integration bet against the TSMC-centered foundry bottleneck. But the two-day stock swing triggered by mere rumor of TSMC's involvement, Intel's unproven 14A node, and SpaceX's own non-binding SEC disclosures together show the market -- and the underlying legal structure -- still treat TSMC's participation as the deciding factor. This keeps leading-edge foundry capacity classified as a Structural Bottleneck; Terafab remains a Growth Area worth continued monitoring, not yet a proven escape route. Intel's and TSMC's Q3 2026 earnings calls are the next real tests.
