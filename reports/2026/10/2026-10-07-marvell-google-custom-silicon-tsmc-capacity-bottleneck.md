# 一句話結論

Marvell's Google-powered $70-90B 2031 target shows design capacity diversifying, but both design houses still compete for the same scarce TSMC packaging capacity.

# Executive Summary

On October 6, 2026, Marvell held its Investor Day and set a first-ever fiscal 2031 revenue target of $70-90 billion, raised its fiscal 2028 outlook to roughly $20 billion, and raised its fiscal 2029 custom-silicon target to $12 billion-plus, leaning on a Google relationship worth up to $120 billion through fiscal 2033. Marvell shares rose 6-7%, and -- notably -- Broadcom shares rose about 4% the same day. This directly engages a kill condition this pipeline recorded on September 5, 2026: a qualified competitor winning a large program away from Broadcom would invalidate the design-bottleneck thesis. The evidence shows partial triggering, not invalidation: Google qualified Marvell for new inference and attach-level silicon while keeping its core Broadcom TPU relationship intact through 2031. The more durable constraint sits upstream: both companies compete for the same limited TSMC CoWoS/advanced-packaging capacity (~127,000 wafers/month by end-2026).

# Research Question / Why Now

**Primary question:** see front matter above.

**Sub-questions:**
- What did Marvell disclose at its October 6, 2026 Investor Day about targets, design-win pipeline, and capacity prepayments?
- Does Marvell's Google deal replace, dilute, or merely supplement Broadcom's existing TPU relationship with Google?
- Did Broadcom's market reaction on and around October 6, 2026 suggest a loss for Broadcom, or an expanding market?
- Is this pipeline's September 5, 2026 kill condition triggered, partially triggered, or not triggered?
- Does TSMC's packaging/wafer capacity remain the more durable Structural Bottleneck underneath both companies' growth?

**Why now:** Marvell's Investor Day, held October 6, 2026, directly tests a specific kill condition this pipeline recorded in its September 5, 2026 Broadcom report.

**Thesis:** Marvell's progress weakens, but does not invalidate, the "one indispensable design house" framing of Broadcom's concentration risk; it reinforces TSMC's shared packaging and wafer capacity as the deeper, more durable bottleneck.

# Known Facts

- Oct 6, 2026: Marvell set a first-ever FY2031 revenue target of $70-90B (~55-60% CAGR) and raised its FY2028 outlook to ~$20B. _(s1, s4, s6)_
- Marvell raised its FY2029 custom-silicon target to $12B-plus; custom silicon is ~$1.5B (~18% of FY26 revenue) today, guided to grow 20%+ in FY27 and more than double in FY28. _(s4, s5)_
- Marvell disclosed 50+ design-win opportunities across 10+ customers (3 XPU wins, 9 XPU-attach programs) and ~$1B in planned capacity prepayments for 3nm wafer/advanced-packaging allocation. _(s4, s5)_
- Marvell shares rose ~6-7% (to ~$287) on Oct 6, 2026; Broadcom shares rose ~4% (to ~$376) the same day. _(s5, s6)_
- Marvell's Google relationship: a July 29, 2026 agreement covering TPU-attached inference accelerators, storage/network/memory-interface controllers and near-memory compute, plus an Aug 18, 2026 warrant (58.97M shares, ~$12.2B, ~7% of Marvell) vesting in 240 tranches toward a $120B ceiling through FY2033. _(s2, s3, s7)_
- April 2026 reporting first disclosed Google-Marvell talks on two new AI chips (a memory-processing unit and an inference-specific TPU), explicitly framed as Google diversifying beyond Broadcom -- days after Broadcom separately locked in a through-2031 Google TPU agreement. _(s8, s9)_
- Broadcom shares fell ~5% on Aug 19, 2026 when the Google-Marvell deal became public (alongside separately cited VMware/financing concerns). _(s10)_
- TSMC's CoWoS capacity is estimated at ~127,000 wafers/month by end-2026; Nvidia books over half annually (800-850K wafers), Broadcom 240K+ (serving Meta/Google TPU), MediaTek newly ~20K. _(s11)_
- Bear-case commentary flags Marvell's customer concentration, a backloaded Google payoff (mostly FY2029+), and prior "lack of stickiness" downgrades. _(s12, s13)_

# Reasonable Inferences

- Because Broadcom's stock rose alongside Marvell's on Oct 6, 2026 -- unlike its ~5% drop when the Google-Marvell deal first became public on Aug 19, 2026 -- the market now reads Marvell's progress as expanding the total custom-silicon market, not reallocating a fixed pool of business away from Broadcom. _(s5, s6, s10)_
- Because Marvell's Google programs concentrate in attach-level and new inference silicon rather than Google's core TPU compute die, and Broadcom's TPU agreement runs through 2031, the September 5, 2026 kill condition is best read as partially triggered (weaken), not fully triggered (invalidate). _(s2, s8, s9)_
- Because both Broadcom (240K+ wafers/year) and Marvell (new $1B capacity prepayment) draw on the same ~127,000-wafer/month TSMC CoWoS pool Nvidia already dominates, a second successful design house intensifies rather than relieves competition for upstream packaging capacity. _(s4, s11)_

# Core Mechanism

Hyperscalers qualify custom-silicon design houses years in advance of volume production, and Google's decision to add Marvell for TPU-attach and new inference silicon -- while keeping Broadcom's core TPU compute-die relationship intact through 2031 -- shows design-house capacity has real, if partial, elasticity: a second credible supplier can be qualified without displacing the incumbent. But both design houses still depend on the same finite TSMC leading-edge wafer and CoWoS packaging pool, which expands slowly because packaging-tool lead times run 3-6 months. As more design houses succeed in winning hyperscaler business, they compete for -- rather than relieve pressure on -- that shared upstream pool, so the bottleneck migrates up the chain rather than disappearing.

TSMC's packaging/wafer layer is reaffirmed as the Structural Bottleneck: capacity reaches only ~127,000 wafers/month by end-2026 and expands slowly. Design-house capacity downgrades further toward a Growth Area: Marvell's credible second-supplier status shows medium, not low, elasticity and substitutability at that layer.

# Strongest Bear Case

- **Competition**: Broadcom retains the deeper, longer-dated Google relationship (through 2031) and a reported ~70% custom-accelerator market share; Marvell's wins are attach-level and new inference silicon, not displacement.
- **Execution**: Analysts flag the bulk of the $120B Google ceiling as backloaded to FY2029+, with prior downgrades citing "lack of stickiness" in custom-silicon wins.
- **Supply**: Both Broadcom and Marvell depend on the same constrained TSMC CoWoS pool; if that capacity fails to expand as projected, both companies' targets -- not just one -- are at risk.
- **Valuation**: Marvell's stock had already priced in substantial optimism ahead of the Investor Day; multiple 2025-2026 analysts flagged valuation above 40x forward earnings as vulnerable to any shortfall.

# Final Assessment

**Verdict: VALIDATE** (confidence: medium)

Marvell's October 6, 2026 Investor Day partially triggers this pipeline's own kill condition for Broadcom's design-capacity bottleneck: a second qualified design house has won real, large hyperscaler business. But Broadcom's core TPU relationship with Google is intact through 2031, and both companies rallied together on the news -- evidence of an expanding market, not displacement. This continues the downgrade of design-house capacity from Structural Bottleneck toward Growth Area while reaffirming TSMC's shared, slow-to-expand wafer and CoWoS packaging capacity as the deeper structural constraint. Broadcom's Q4 FY2026 call and TSMC's Q3 2026 call are the next real tests.
