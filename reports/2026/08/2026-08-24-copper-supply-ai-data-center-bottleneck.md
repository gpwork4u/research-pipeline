# 一句話結論

Copper's mine-supply rigidity is a genuine long-run bottleneck, but the current price spike looks more tariff- and DRC-driven than AI-caused.

# Executive Summary

Copper prices topped $14,000/tonne through August 2026, near the January 2026 record, as a tariff-anticipation squeeze pulled COMEX inventories roughly eightfold higher and the DRC banned copper and cobalt concentrate exports on August 6. JPMorgan estimates AI data centers will draw 475,000 tonnes of copper in 2026, up from 110,000 in 2025 -- but that is under 2% of the ~28.7-million-tonne global refined market. Forecasters disagree sharply on the overall balance: ICSG flipped from a 150,000-tonne deficit to a 96,000-tonne surplus forecast for the same year. What is not contested is supply rigidity: new mine production takes an average 17.9 years.

# Research Question / Why Now

**Primary question:** see front matter above.

**Sub-questions:**
- Why do 2026 supply-demand forecasts (ICSG, Wood Mackenzie, S&P Global, JPMorgan) diverge so sharply?
- How large is AI data center demand relative to the total refined copper market?
- Does copper mining/refining meet the strict Structural Bottleneck test versus CoWoS, ABF substrates, HBM and grid transformers?
- What supply-side responses -- new mines, recycling, aluminum substitution, trade policy -- are underway?
- What would falsify the thesis that copper is an independent AI-buildout bottleneck?

**Why now:** Copper traded near its all-time high through August 2026; the DRC banned copper/cobalt concentrate exports August 6; a tariff-anticipation squeeze pulled COMEX inventories roughly eightfold higher since February 2025; Freeport-McMoRan's July 23 Q2 results showed continued capacity investment; and competing 2026 forecasts range from a 150,000-tonne deficit to a 96,000-tonne surplus -- a physical-commodity node this pipeline has not yet examined.

**Thesis:** Near-record copper prices, the DRC export ban, and tariff-driven US stockpiling indicate copper has joined chip packaging, substrates, memory and grid transformers as a physical AI-buildout constraint, but the AI-specific causal case is weaker than for those prior nodes: AI data centers are under 2% of global refined demand, and ICSG's own forecast flipped from deficit to surplus for the same year. What is uncontested is mine-supply rigidity -- an average 17.9-year discovery-to-production timeline -- a genuinely low-elasticity, hard-to-substitute (for mission-critical power distribution) upstream node that leaves little room to absorb any sustained AI-linked demand growth.

# Known Facts

- Copper hit $14,369.50/tonne on Aug 6, 2026 after the DRC export ban, near the Jan 29 all-time peak of $14,527.50/tonne. _(evidence: ev-7, ev-11)_
- A tariff-anticipation squeeze pulled 200,000+ tonnes into US ports in July 2026 (largest month since 2014); COMEX inventories rose ~8x since Feb 2025 to ~650,000 tonnes; LME stock fell to ~218,000-223,000 tonnes. _(evidence: ev-8)_
- DRC banned copper/cobalt concentrate exports effective Aug 6, 2026; Q1 2026 DRC exports were 696,725 tonnes of cathodes vs. just 53,926 tonnes of concentrate. _(evidence: ev-7)_
- ICSG's Oct 2025 forecast projected a 150,000-tonne 2026 deficit; its Apr 2026 forecast reversed to a 96,000-tonne surplus for the same year. _(evidence: ev-2, ev-3)_
- S&P Global (Jan 2026): copper supply to fall ~10 million tonnes short of demand by 2040; demand to reach 42 million tonnes (+50%), defense demand to triple. _(evidence: ev-1)_
- Wood Mackenzie: 304,000-tonne 2025 refined deficit, wider gap in 2026; only ~70% of 2035 demand meetable; AI grid needs ~1.1M tonnes/year by 2030. A more recent outlook shifted toward balance. _(evidence: ev-6)_
- JPMorgan: AI data center copper demand ~475,000 tonnes in 2026, up from ~110,000 in 2025; economy-wide 2026 shortfall projected at 330,000 tonnes. _(evidence: ev-5)_
- Freeport-McMoRan Q2 2026: revenue $7.029bn (beat), adjusted EPS $0.74 (beat by 21%), capex $1.1bn; Grasberg output rose to 69,000 tons/day; shares fell on capex concerns. _(evidence: ev-4)_
- Average copper mine discovery-to-production timeline: 17.9 years (+40%); permitting alone stretching toward ~30 years in some jurisdictions vs. 5-7 years in the 1990s. _(evidence: ev-9)_
- Aluminum substitutes for copper only in non-critical circuits: needs ~1.6x the cross-section, forms an oxide layer, expands ~40% more; copper remains preferred for mission-critical main power distribution. _(evidence: ev-10)_
- Chile, the top producer, was expected to see 2026 output decline from ongoing mine disruptions. _(evidence: ev-8)_

# Reasonable Inferences

- ICSG's own deficit-to-surplus reversal within six months, against Wood Mackenzie's and S&P Global's longer-horizon deficit view, suggests near-term headlines conflate a genuine long-run tightening with a shorter-run, tariff- and DRC-driven price spike. _(evidence: ev-2, ev-3, ev-7, ev-8)_
- AI's 475,000 tonnes is under 2% of the ~28.7M-tonne market, so AI is unlikely to be the primary driver of the price spike, even as its ~4.3x year-over-year growth compounds onto already-tight conditions. _(evidence: ev-2, ev-5)_
- With mine supply averaging 17.9 years and permitting stretching toward 30 years, the mining/refining node has essentially no near-term capacity to respond to demand growth, unlike faster-scaling fabrication or recycling nodes. _(evidence: ev-9)_
- Aluminum substitution likely blunts rather than eliminates copper demand growth, since copper remains preferred for the highest-value, mission-critical use case. _(evidence: ev-10)_
- The DRC ban targets concentrate (a small share of DRC exports versus finished cathodes), so its direct refined-supply effect may be smaller than the 1.8% price reaction suggests. _(evidence: ev-7)_

# Core Mechanism

AI data centers are a small (under 2%) but fast-growing (~4.3x year over year) slice of a ~28.7-million-tonne global refined copper market whose short-term balance forecasts have proven volatile -- ICSG itself flipped from a 150,000-tonne deficit to a 96,000-tonne surplus for the same calendar year. The August 2026 price spike toward record levels looks driven more directly by a tariff-anticipation stockpiling rush and the DRC's new concentrate export ban than by AI demand specifically. What does not depend on any single forecast, however, is supply-side rigidity: new copper mine production takes an average 17.9 years from discovery, with permitting alone stretching toward 30 years in some jurisdictions -- meaning that regardless of this year's balance, the mining and refining node has structurally low capacity to respond quickly, and aluminum substitution only partially offsets this for non-mission-critical applications.

# Bottleneck Analysis

| Node | Demand | Supply elasticity | Substitutability | Pricing power | Opportunity | Classification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Copper mine production and refining capacity | Extreme | Low | Hard | High | 4 | Structural Bottleneck |

| Secondary (scrap) copper recycling | High | Medium | Medium | Low | 2 | Growth Area |

- **Mining/refining**: Demand extreme (50% growth to 2040 per S&P Global); elasticity low (17.9-year average timeline, permitting toward 30 years); substitutability hard for mission-critical power distribution -- meets the strict test.
- **Fabrication**: New cable/busbar capacity can be added in 1-3 years and aluminum offers a partial substitute, so elasticity and substitutability are only medium -- Growth Area, not a bottleneck.
- **Recycling**: The most elastic near-term lever (ICSG cites 5.7% secondary growth in 2027) but gated by decades-long scrap-stream lag -- Growth Area.

# Final Assessment

**Verdict: VALIDATE** (confidence: medium)

Copper mining and refining meets this research's strict Structural Bottleneck test on supply-side fundamentals alone: an average 17.9-year discovery-to-production timeline and permitting delays stretching toward 30 years leave essentially no near-term capacity response, and copper remains hard to substitute for mission-critical power distribution. But the AI-specific causal story is weaker than for the CoWoS, ABF-substrate, HBM or grid-transformer bottlenecks this pipeline has previously examined: AI data centers are under 2% of global refined demand, and the current price spike toward record levels looks substantially driven by tariff-anticipation stockpiling and the DRC's new export ban rather than AI demand. The thesis merits continued validation rather than a stronger call, given how quickly the leading forecaster's own balance estimate reversed.
