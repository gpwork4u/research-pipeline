# 一句話結論

PJM's new curtailment-priority rule doesn't create the bottleneck -- it rations a pre-existing, decade-scale scarcity of firm US generation capacity that gas-turbine lead times cannot quickly fix.

# Executive Summary

PJM, the largest US grid operator, filed a proposal with FERC on August 13, 2026 that would curtail new 50MW-plus data centers before ordinary customers during shortages unless they secure their own generation, contracts, or purchased capacity ("BYONC"). This follows FERC's June 2026 order compelling all six US grid operators to reform large-load rules, and lands as PJM's capacity-auction price hit a record $333.44/MW-day and the grid missed its reliability target for the first time. The real constraint sits upstream: gas-turbine backlogs at GE Vernova, Siemens Energy and Mitsubishi Power now run three to five years. The rule doesn't bind until June 2027, and PJM previously softened a similar mandatory proposal after pushback, so its final, binding form remains genuinely contested.

# Research Question / Why Now

**Primary question:** see front matter above.

**Sub-questions:**
- What does PJM's August 13, 2026 IRAS filing require of new 50MW+ loads, and what qualifies as BYONC?
- How does this policy layer relate to physical grid-equipment scarcity and gas-turbine backlogs?
- How large is PJM's capacity-price run-up and reliability shortfall, and how much data-center demand is real versus "phantom"?
- Is this a PJM-only move, or is FERC's broader order producing similar rules elsewhere?
- What would falsify the thesis, given PJM's history of softening an earlier mandatory proposal?

**Why now:** PJM filed IRAS and a Large Load Registry with FERC on August 13, 2026, requesting an October 12, 2026 effective date, days after Anthropic's $45 billion Nscale power deal and amid a record capacity-auction price -- a distinct, non-duplicative angle on the AI power constraint versus this pipeline's 2026-08-23 grid-transformer-equipment report.

**Thesis:** PJM's curtailment-priority filing is an allocation mechanism, not the underlying cause of scarcity: the true bottleneck is firm, dispatchable generation capacity, revealed by a more-than-tenfold capacity-price rise and PJM's first-ever reliability shortfall, while gas-turbine lead times gate the self-generation path the rule is meant to encourage.

# Known Facts

- FERC's June 18, 2026 show-cause orders (Docket RM26-4) gave all six US grid operators 60 days to justify or reform large-load rules, covering ~200 million people. _(evidence: ev-1, ev-2)_
- PJM's Board directed the filing July 27, 2026; PJM filed IRAS and a Large Load Registry with FERC (Docket ER26-3380) on August 13, 2026. _(evidence: ev-3, ev-4, ev-5, ev-18)_
- IRAS defines a "New Large Load" as 50MW+ at one site connecting after June 1, 2027; uncovered demand is curtailed before ordinary demand-response, unless backed by BYONC (on-site generation, a long-term contract, or purchased PJM capacity). _(evidence: ev-5, ev-6, ev-7, ev-8)_
- PJM requested an October 12, 2026 tariff effective date. _(evidence: ev-6, ev-9)_
- PJM's capacity-auction price rose from $28.92/MW-day (2024/25) to a record $333.44/MW-day (2027/28); data centers were ~40% ($6.5bn of $16.4bn) of the latest auction's cleared cost. _(evidence: ev-10, ev-12)_
- The 2027/28 auction cleared roughly 6,623MW short of PJM's reliability requirement -- reportedly its first-ever shortfall. _(evidence: ev-11)_
- GE Vernova's gas-power backlog reached 116GW (Q2 2026, up from 83GW at end-2025); turbines ordered today deliver no earlier than 2031. Siemens Energy's firm backlog was 69GW; Mitsubishi Power's was 35GW, up from 23GW a year earlier. _(evidence: ev-13, ev-14)_
- Wood Mackenzie estimates only ~28% of the 1,066GW of requested US data-center power will actually be committed. _(evidence: ev-15)_
- ERCOT separately approved curtailment-acceptance rules for large loads June 18, 2026; it received 198GW of interconnection requests in Q1 2026 alone. _(evidence: ev-16)_
- Anthropic agreed to pay Nscale $45 billion over six years for 460MW of capacity in West Virginia, announced August 26, 2026. _(evidence: ev-17)_
- PJM earlier retreated from a mandatory bring-your-own-generation proposal to a voluntary one after 2025 stakeholder pushback. _(evidence: ev-19)_

# Reasonable Inferences

- PJM's record capacity price and first-ever reliability shortfall indicate the scarcity predates and is independent of the IRAS filing, which rations rather than creates supply. _(evidence: ev-10, ev-11)_
- Because BYONC also accepts contracts or purchased capacity, most New Large Loads will likely satisfy it without building new turbines, given 3-5 year OEM lead times. _(evidence: ev-5, ev-13, ev-14)_
- If ~72% of requested power is "phantom load," PJM's queue and shortfall figures may embed some inflation, though the price and shortfall data indicate a real shortfall exists today. _(evidence: ev-15, ev-11)_
- PJM's prior mandatory-to-voluntary retreat plus the pending FERC approval and June 2027 binding date leave real room for the rule to be softened before it constrains any project. _(evidence: ev-6, ev-19)_

# Core Mechanism

PJM's capacity-auction price rose more than tenfold in three years and its 2027/28 auction failed for the first time to clear enough capacity to meet its own reliability requirement -- evidence that firm, dispatchable generation is genuinely scarce, independent of any single filing. Gas-turbine lead times at the three dominant OEMs (GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Mitsubishi Power) now run three to five years, so new on-site generation cannot quickly absorb new AI data-center load. PJM's IRAS/BYONC filing responds to this by making new 50MW+ loads first in line for curtailment unless they secure capacity through generation, contracts, or purchases -- shifting risk onto developers rather than expanding supply. Because BYONC accepts contracts and purchased capacity, not only new turbines, the rule mostly reallocates existing scarce capacity toward better-capitalized developers (illustrated by Anthropic's $45bn Nscale deal) rather than directly triggering new physical build.

# Bottleneck Analysis

Applying the strict test, only firm generation capacity itself qualifies as a structural bottleneck; the policy and self-generation layers are growth areas with partial substitutes.

| Node | Demand | Supply elasticity | Substitutability | Pricing power | Opportunity | Classification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Firm generation capacity | Extreme | Low | Hard | High | 5 | Structural Bottleneck |
| Interconnection/tariff admin (IRAS, RM26-4) | High | Medium | Medium | Medium | 3 | Growth Area |
| Behind-the-meter self-generation | Extreme | Low | Medium | High | 4 | Growth Area |

- **Generation**: extreme demand, low elasticity, hard substitutability -- meets the strict test.
- **Interconnection**: high demand but medium elasticity (FERC can rule within months) and medium substitutability (developers can pick another RTO) -- Growth Area.
- **Self-generation**: extreme demand but medium substitutability, since BYONC also accepts contracts or purchased capacity -- Growth Area.

# Strongest Bear Case

- **Regulation**: PJM already softened a mandatory bring-your-own-generation proposal once after 2025 pushback; FERC could modify or reject the current filing too.
- **Demand**: Wood Mackenzie estimates ~72% of requested US data-center power is "phantom load" that won't materialize.
- **Execution**: The rule doesn't bind until June 2027 and only covers new loads, leaving roughly ten months to secure grandfathering or BYONC.
- **Supply**: BYONC accepts contracts or purchased capacity, so turbine lead times don't necessarily gate compliance for most developers.
- **Competition**: Developers can route new loads to other RTOs or off-grid sites, arbitraging PJM-specific policy risk.

# Final Assessment

**Verdict: VALIDATE** (confidence: medium)

Firm generation capacity within PJM meets this research's strict Structural Bottleneck test: extreme and growing demand, low supply elasticity given multi-year turbine lead times, and hard substitutability for grid-reliability purposes. PJM's IRAS/BYONC filing is best understood as a rationing response to that pre-existing scarcity rather than its cause, and because BYONC accepts contracts and purchased capacity, its near-term effect looks more like reallocation toward well-capitalized developers than a direct trigger for new generation. The filing's durability is genuinely unresolved: FERC has not yet ruled, the binding mechanism doesn't apply until June 2027, and PJM has a track record of softening similar proposals -- warranting continued validation against the FERC docket and auction data rather than a stronger call.
