# 一句話結論

Helium is a genuine, non-substitutable AI-fab input squeezed by a war-damaged Qatari supply and a closed Strait of Hormuz, but leading-edge fabs' buffers and 2027 normalization guidance temper its durability.

# Executive Summary

Iranian strikes on Qatar's Ras Laffan complex beginning around March 2026 cut Qatar's LNG export capacity by 17% and, by QatarEnergy's own estimate, could take up to five years to fully repair. The Strait of Hormuz -- Qatar's only maritime helium export route -- remained at a small fraction of normal transit volume as of Lloyd's List Intelligence's August 19, 2026 brief. Air Products and Linde both flagged helium as a margin headwind in their July 2026 quarterly results even as their electronics/semiconductor backlogs hit records on AI-driven demand. TSMC, Samsung and SK Hynix hold multi-month buffers and high recycling rates that blunt near-term impact, and Linde does not expect the market to normalize before early 2027. The main uncertainty is how much of global helium supply is actually impaired -- estimates range from 11% to 33-35%.

# Research Question / Why Now

**Primary question:** see front matter above.

**Sub-questions:**
- How large is the actual disruption to global helium supply, and how much is logistics (Hormuz) versus lost production (Ras Laffan damage)?
- How exposed are leading-edge fabs (TSMC) versus memory makers (Samsung, SK Hynix) versus smaller fabs, given differing buffers?
- What is the financial impact on industrial gas majors (Air Products, Linde), and are they capturing pricing power or absorbing costs?
- What is helium's true supply elasticity versus the multi-year timelines in this pipeline's other bottleneck reports?
- What would falsify the thesis that this is durable rather than a war-contingent shock?

**Why now:** QatarEnergy's own statements describe a 17% LNG-capacity cut and up to five years to repair Ras Laffan; the Strait of Hormuz remained near-closed as of the August 19, 2026 Lloyd's List brief; Air Products' July 30 and Linde's July 31, 2026 results both cited helium as a margin headwind alongside record electronics backlogs. This is a distinct upstream-materials bottleneck this pipeline has not yet examined, separate from its prior packaging, substrate, memory, grid-transformer, copper and optical-transceiver reports.

**Thesis:** Helium is a genuine structural bottleneck behind AI-driven semiconductor fabrication -- a non-substitutable input to crystal growth, cooling and leak detection, concentrated in a war-damaged Qatari complex with a five-year repair estimate and a closed export strait. Unlike this pipeline's prior AI-demand-driven bottlenecks, this is a supply-side geopolitical shock that AI/electronics demand happens to be absorbing fastest. Its durability is genuinely uncertain given fab-level buffers and Linde's early-2027 normalization guidance.

# Known Facts

- QatarEnergy declared force majeure on LNG contracts after Iranian strikes on Ras Laffan beginning ~March 2, 2026 (further strikes March 18-19); its own statement cites a 17% export-capacity cut, ~$20bn estimated annual revenue loss, and up to five years to repair. _(evidence: ev-1, ev-2)_
- Qatar normally supplies roughly a third of world helium; independent estimates of the affected share range from ~11% to 33-35% of global supply. _(evidence: ev-3, ev-12)_
- As of mid-to-late August 2026, the Strait of Hormuz remained near-closed to commercial shipping: ~3 ships transited on one day versus ~85/day normally. _(evidence: ev-13)_
- Industry commentators labeled the event "Helium Shortage 5.0" -- the fifth and, by consensus, most severe supply-side helium crisis in two decades. _(evidence: ev-15)_
- TSMC holds an estimated 3-6 months of helium inventory with 80-90% recycling recovery at leading fabs; Samsung and SK Hynix were reported to hold ~4-6 months of buffer. _(evidence: ev-6, ev-7)_
- Air Products' fiscal Q3 2026 adjusted EPS was $3.47 (+12% YoY, beating guidance); full-year guidance raised to $13.39-$13.49; helium was cited as a margin headwind, partly offset by Asia electronics demand. _(evidence: ev-8, ev-9)_
- Linde's Q2 2026 sales were a record $9.3bn, EPS $4.5, 29.5% operating margin; sale-of-gas backlog hit a record $8.1bn on a $1bn electronics win; helium was dollar-accretive but margin-dilutive, with normalization not expected before early 2027. _(evidence: ev-10, ev-11)_
- Russia's Amur plant brought a second line online in late 2025, selling helium to China below pre-crisis Qatari rates; Russia imposed helium export controls (through end-2027) in April 2026. _(evidence: ev-14)_

# Reasonable Inferences

- QatarEnergy's own 5-year repair estimate plus a still-collapsed Hormuz transit volume suggest helium supply likely stays structurally tighter than pre-2026 levels through at least 2027, regardless of when hostilities end. _(evidence: ev-1, ev-4, ev-13)_
- Because Air Products and Linde both describe helium as margin-neutral-to-dilutive despite higher prices, pricing power appears concentrated upstream or dissipated in logistics costs, not captured by distributors -- unlike the component-level margin capture seen in this pipeline's packaging, substrate and memory reports. _(evidence: ev-8, ev-9, ev-10, ev-11)_
- Multi-month buffers and high recycling at TSMC, Samsung and SK Hynix mean the shortage likely binds later and more weakly on AI-relevant chip output than on general industrial and medical helium users. _(evidence: ev-6, ev-7)_
- Russia's Amur supply and its own export controls likely leave non-China global helium tighter for longer than Chinese domestic supply, echoing supply-chain regionalization patterns seen elsewhere in this pipeline. _(evidence: ev-14)_

# Core Mechanism

Helium is recovered only as a byproduct of specific natural-gas fields and is irreplaceable in semiconductor wafer crystal growth, plasma processing, cryogenic cooling and leak detection -- no substitute exists at qualifying purity. Roughly a third of world supply flows through Qatar's Ras Laffan complex, which Iranian strikes damaged in March 2026; QatarEnergy itself estimates up to five years to repair, and the Strait of Hormuz, the sole maritime export route, has been collapsed for months with no resolution date. This differs from this pipeline's prior AI-demand-driven bottlenecks (CoWoS, HBM, ABF substrates): AI is not the cause of scarcity here, it is simply the fastest-growing demand segment absorbing an externally caused supply shock, evidenced by Air Products' and Linde's record electronics backlogs sitting alongside helium-specific margin headwinds rather than windfalls.

# Strongest Bear Case

- **Supply**: TSMC, Samsung and SK Hynix hold 3-6 months of buffer inventory and 80-90% recycling, limiting near-term AI-chip production impact.
- **Demand**: MRI, aerospace and industrial uses dominate total helium demand; electronics is only the fastest-growing segment, so "AI bottleneck" framing may overstate AI's causal role.
- **Economics**: Linde expects normalization by early 2027, and both majors already describe helium as margin-neutral-to-dilutive rather than a windfall.
- **Competition**: Russia's Amur plant and ExxonMobil's LaBarge field provide partial substitute supply, even with Russia's own export controls limiting reach.
- **Execution**: Neither major has converted the shortage into clear margin expansion, suggesting value capture sits further upstream or is lost to logistics costs.

# Final Assessment

**Verdict: VALIDATE** (confidence: medium)

Helium meets this research's strict Structural Bottleneck test at the extraction layer: high and growing electronics-driven demand, low supply elasticity concentrated in a war-damaged Qatari complex with a multi-year repair estimate, and hard substitutability for semiconductor-grade cryogenic use. But this bottleneck differs from this pipeline's prior AI-caused constraints -- it is a geopolitical supply shock that AI demand happens to be absorbing fastest -- and its durability is genuinely contested: leading-edge fabs' multi-month buffers and high recycling rates blunt near-term impact, and Linde's own guidance points to early-2027 normalization. The thesis merits continued validation against Hormuz shipping data and Ras Laffan repair progress rather than a stronger call.
