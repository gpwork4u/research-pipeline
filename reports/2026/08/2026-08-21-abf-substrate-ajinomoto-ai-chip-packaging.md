# 一句話結論

As TSMC's CoWoS packaging capacity closes in on demand, the binding constraint on AI accelerator supply is shifting one layer upstream to ABF dielectric film and the substrates built from it -- a genuine structural bottleneck by this research's own strict test, where CoWoS assembly increasingly no longer qualifies.

# Executive Summary

At the OCP APAC Summit on August 11, 2026, TSMC Vice President Jun He said CoWoS advanced-packaging capacity has nearly doubled every year for three consecutive years and is now "very close" to market demand -- while explicitly naming memory and ABF substrates as the AI supply chain's next bottlenecks. That statement lands alongside a concrete set of developments: Ajinomoto, which holds an estimated 95%+ share of the global market for ABF (Ajinomoto Build-up Film), the core dielectric material used in essentially all high-performance IC package substrates, notified substrate makers of a 30% price increase effective Q3 2026; Ibiden disclosed a roughly $3.3 billion, three-year capital investment plan (FY2026-2028) after stating that AI-server substrate demand continues to exceed its capacity; and Taiwan's Unimicron raised its 2026 capital expenditure to roughly $1.7 billion even as rising depreciation compressed its gross margin to 13.2% in Q2 2026. Applying this research's strict Structural Bottleneck test (demand High/Extreme, supply elasticity Low, substitutability Hard), the ABF film and ABF-based substrate nodes both qualify, while CoWoS assembly itself -- given TSMC's own account of capacity nearly tripling over three years -- is reclassified here as a Growth Area rather than a Structural Bottleneck. The main uncertainty is whether the multi-year capacity investments already underway at Ajinomoto, Ibiden, and Unimicron will resolve this constraint on a similar multi-year timeline to how CoWoS itself eased, or whether simultaneous capacity landing across suppliers could instead flip today's shortage into oversupply, as has happened before in adjacent memory and packaging cycles.

# Research Question / Why Now

**Primary question:** Now that TSMC's CoWoS packaging capacity is reportedly closing in on demand, does the AI accelerator supply chain's binding structural constraint sit one layer upstream -- in Ajinomoto's near-monopoly on ABF dielectric film and the multi-year capacity build-out required at IC substrate makers such as Ibiden and Unimicron?

**Sub-questions:**
- What is the actual state of CoWoS capacity relative to demand in mid-to-late 2026, and does the narrowing gap support the claim that packaging assembly itself is no longer the binding constraint?
- What is Ajinomoto's market position in ABF dielectric film, and what does its Q3 2026 price increase and new-plant timeline reveal about the elasticity of ABF film supply?
- How much capital and time are IC substrate makers (Ibiden, Unimicron, and peers) committing to expand ABF substrate capacity, and when does that capacity actually come online?
- Does the ABF film/substrate node meet the strict Structural Bottleneck qualification (demand High/Extreme + supply elasticity Low + substitutability Hard), or is it better characterized as a Growth Area?
- What would falsify the thesis that ABF materials and substrates -- rather than CoWoS packaging capacity -- are now the binding constraint on AI accelerator supply?

**Why now:** TSMC Vice President Jun He's August 11, 2026 statement at the OCP APAC Summit that CoWoS advanced-packaging capacity is now "very close" to market demand after tripling-plus over three years, while explicitly flagging memory and ABF substrates as the AI supply chain's next bottlenecks -- combined with Ajinomoto's Q3 2026 30% ABF dielectric film price increase (Ajinomoto holds an estimated 95%+ global share) and multi-billion-dollar, multi-year ABF substrate capacity-expansion announcements from Ibiden (~500 billion yen / ~$3.3B, FY2026-2028) and Unimicron (2026 capex raised to ~NT$53.7B / ~$1.7B).

**Thesis:** As TSMC's CoWoS advanced-packaging capacity closes in on demand after tripling-plus over three years, the AI accelerator supply chain's binding structural constraint is shifting one layer upstream to ABF dielectric film and the IC substrates built from it: Ajinomoto's estimated 95%+ share of global ABF film supply, its Q3 2026 30% price increase, and multi-year, multi-billion-dollar capacity build-outs at substrate makers Ibiden and Unimicron (each targeting 2028 or later for major new capacity) together indicate that packaging is no longer the tightest link -- materials and substrate fabrication now are, and by the strict definition used in this research (extreme demand, low supply elasticity, hard substitutability), this node qualifies as a genuine structural bottleneck where CoWoS assembly increasingly does not.

# Known Facts

- At the OCP APAC Summit on August 11, 2026, TSMC Vice President of Advanced Packaging Technology and Services Jun He stated that CoWoS capacity has nearly doubled every year for three consecutive years and is now "very close" to market demand; TSMC's 5.5x-reticle CoWoS is in volume production with yields consistently topping 98% across multiple AI customer products and reaching 99% on some, and the company plans to scale beyond 14x reticle size CoWoS by 2029. _(evidence: ev-1, ev-2)_
- At the same event, Jun He identified memory and ABF substrates as the AI supply chain's key emerging bottlenecks, stating the industry is likely to face tight ABF substrate supply over the next few years, with the constraint moving from inside the CoWoS packaging process itself to the surrounding components; he noted that as capacity tightens, procurement teams are turning to multiple sourcing, but variations in different suppliers' mechanical and thermal properties are making process control more difficult. _(evidence: ev-1, ev-2)_
- On TSMC's Q2 2026 earnings call (July 16, 2026), CEO C.C. Wei said CoWoS remains the majority advanced-packaging technology today, that TSMC is building a pilot line for an alternative, lower-cost packaging technology that will take about another year to mature before customer production, and that TSMC raised its full-year 2026 capital budget to between $60 billion and $64 billion given continued structural AI demand. _(evidence: ev-3)_
- Ajinomoto, which holds an estimated 95%+ share of the global market for ABF (Ajinomoto Build-up Film) -- the core dielectric insulating material used in essentially all high-performance IC package substrates -- notified Taiwanese and other substrate manufacturers of a 30% price increase on its core ABF film, effective in Q3 2026; industry reporting links the increase to an ABF substrate supply-demand gap that is expected to persist through 2027. _(evidence: ev-4, ev-8, ev-11)_
- Ibiden's board approved a capital investment plan of approximately 500 billion yen (roughly $3.3 billion) over the three fiscal years from FY2026 through FY2028 to expand production of high-performance IC package substrates, with the first phase directed at additional investment in the existing Gama Plant (Cell 6); the company disclosed, in its own February 3, 2026 announcement, that demand for its AI-server IC package substrates continues to exceed capacity and estimated its market share for this product category at roughly 70-80%. _(evidence: ev-5, ev-10)_
- Ibiden funded part of this expansion through a follow-on equity offering of approximately 52.47 billion yen (roughly 6.87 million common shares priced at 7,632 yen each), disclosed alongside its February 2026 capital investment plan. _(evidence: ev-5)_
- On its FY2026 Q2 earnings call (July 29, 2026), Taiwan-listed Unimicron (TWSE: 3037) reported consolidated revenue of roughly NT$27.88 billion (about $860 million), up 5.6% quarter over quarter, while gross margin fell 3.1 percentage points to 13.2% due to rising depreciation expense from capacity expansion and continued price pressure on lower-end ABF and traditional PCB lines; the board approved an additional NT$19.7 billion (about $609 million) of capital expenditure, bringing 2026 total capex to roughly NT$53.7 billion (about $1.7 billion). _(evidence: ev-6)_
- Unimicron reported that AI and HPC applications accounted for more than 60% of its ABF substrate revenue in the first half of 2026, projected to rise toward roughly 70% in the second half, as the company targets record full-year 2026 revenue driven by ABF and AI system-board demand. _(evidence: ev-7)_
- The ABF substrate supply-demand gap is reported to persist through 2027, with at least one industry-analyst estimate suggesting it could widen to as much as 26-29% by 2028, prompting multiple brokerages to raise price targets on ABF substrate makers including Unimicron, Nan Ya PCB, and Kinsus. _(evidence: ev-8, ev-9)_
- A separate TrendForce report (August 19, 2026) described spillover demand from the CoWoS capacity crunch benefiting alternative advanced-packaging approaches such as Intel's EMIB-T, with substrate and packaging suppliers Unimicron and ASE positioned to benefit from that spillover. _(evidence: ev-12)_
- Ibiden's and Unimicron's own disclosures are corroborated in general direction by discovery-tier commentary describing a small number of suppliers (Ibiden, Unimicron, Shinko Electric, AT&S) as controlling most of the high-end IC substrate market with high utilization, though this research did not obtain a secondary- or primary-sourced figure for the exact collective market-share or utilization-rate percentages. _(evidence: ev-5, ev-6, ev-9, ev-10)_

# Reasonable Inferences

- Because TSMC's own VP publicly stated CoWoS capacity is now "very close" to demand after nearly doubling for three consecutive years, and because TSMC's CEO described CoWoS as still the majority technology while actively developing lower-cost alternatives, the CoWoS packaging-assembly step itself appears to be transitioning from a structural bottleneck toward a Growth Area -- consistent with the uncertainty already flagged in this pipeline's August 19, 2026 CoWoS report, where TSMC's stated capacity-ramp target for late 2026 was noted as capable of exceeding then-current full-year demand estimates. _(evidence: ev-1, ev-2, ev-3)_
- Because Ajinomoto's ABF film sits upstream of every major IC substrate maker and commands an estimated 95%+ share with no qualified substitute material in volume production, and because it exercised pricing power with a 30% increase even as substrate makers' own margins are compressing (Unimicron's gross margin fell to 13.2%), Ajinomoto's position looks like the more concentrated and durable chokepoint in this chain, more so than the substrate fabrication step itself, where at least three sizeable competitors (Ibiden, Unimicron, Shinko) provide some degree of multi-sourcing. _(evidence: ev-4, ev-5, ev-6, ev-10, ev-11)_
- Because Ibiden's and Unimicron's expansion plans are both multi-year programs (Ibiden's three-year, ~$3.3B plan through FY2028; Unimicron's capacity additions phased through 2028-2030) and TrendForce-linked analysis projects the ABF substrate supply-demand gap widening through 2027-2028 rather than closing, the substrate-fabrication layer of this chain appears to have materially longer lead times than CoWoS packaging assembly did during its own multi-year build-out, suggesting the AI accelerator supply chain's binding constraint is likely to remain upstream of packaging assembly for at least the next 1-2 years. _(evidence: ev-5, ev-6, ev-9)_
- Because TSMC's own management is simultaneously developing an alternative, lower-cost packaging pilot line (targeted to mature within about a year) and pursuing glass-substrate development, the industry itself appears to be treating ABF-based substrate dependency as a risk worth engineering around, which supports classifying the ABF material/substrate node as a genuine structural constraint rather than simply cyclical tightness that participants expect to self-resolve through capacity additions alone. _(evidence: ev-3)_

# Core Mechanism

TSMC's CoWoS advanced-packaging capacity has nearly tripled over three years and, by its own VP's account, is now close enough to demand that the company describes the AI supply chain's binding constraint as having moved past packaging assembly itself, into memory and ABF substrates. That shift traces to a genuine chokepoint one layer further upstream: Ajinomoto's ABF (Ajinomoto Build-up Film) is the dielectric material used in essentially every high-performance IC package substrate, and Ajinomoto holds an estimated 95%+ share of that market with no qualified substitute reported in volume production. When Ajinomoto pushed through a 30% price increase for Q3 2026, substrate makers had no meaningful alternative supplier to shift toward -- pricing power that shows up starkly downstream, where Unimicron's gross margin fell to 13.2% in Q2 2026 even as its AI-related revenue climbed toward a record year, because rising input costs and capacity-expansion depreciation are compressing margin faster than AI-driven volume can offset it. The substrate-fabrication layer directly beneath Ajinomoto -- Ibiden, Unimicron, and peers -- is itself capital- and time-constrained: Ibiden's roughly $3.3 billion, three-year expansion plan and Unimicron's raised 2026 capex are both multi-year commitments, and industry-analyst projections describe the ABF substrate supply-demand gap persisting through 2027 and potentially widening into 2028. That is a materially longer runway than the three years it took CoWoS capacity to go from constrained to "very close" to demand, and it is why this research locates today's binding constraint one layer upstream of where it sat in this pipeline's prior CoWoS-focused analysis: not in the ability to assemble AI accelerators, but in the ability to make and supply the dielectric-film-based substrates that those accelerators are built on.

# Bottleneck Analysis

| Node | Demand | Supply elasticity | Substitutability | Pricing power | Opportunity | Classification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ABF dielectric film material supply (Ajinomoto) | Extreme | Low | Hard | High | 4 | Structural Bottleneck |

- **ABF dielectric film material supply (Ajinomoto)**: Demand for ABF film is extreme, tracking the entire AI accelerator packaging buildout. Supply elasticity is low: Ajinomoto holds an estimated 95%+ share with no qualified volume substitute material, and its own newly announced capacity is not targeted for production until 2032. Substitutability is hard: every major IC substrate maker (Ibiden, Unimicron, Shinko, AT&S) depends on the same material, and reported process-control difficulties when multi-sourcing alternative dielectric materials indicate switching is not straightforward. Ajinomoto's ability to push through a 30% price increase even as downstream substrate makers' margins compress is direct evidence of pricing power consistent with a genuine chokepoint.
- **High-performance ABF-based IC substrate fabrication (Ibiden, Unimicron, Shinko, AT&S)**: Demand growth is extreme, reflected in Ibiden's and Unimicron's own disclosures that demand exceeds current capacity and both companies' record capital-expenditure programs. Supply elasticity is low given multi-year capex cycles and reported utilization already near capacity at core suppliers. Substitutability is hard in practice near-term: while three to four sizeable suppliers exist, TSMC's own VP noted that variations in different suppliers' mechanical and thermal properties complicate multi-sourcing and process control, meaning buyers cannot easily and quickly substitute between qualified suppliers. Profit capture appears comparatively low: Unimicron's gross margin fell to 13.2% even amid record revenue, consistent with substrate makers absorbing rising input costs (including Ajinomoto's price increase) and depreciation from capacity expansion rather than capturing outsized margin.
- **Advanced packaging / CoWoS assembly capacity (TSMC)**: Demand remains extreme and substitutability for leading-edge CoWoS remains hard (TSMC has no equivalent-scale competitor at this node), which would historically have qualified this as a structural bottleneck -- as this pipeline's August 19, 2026 report found. However, supply elasticity has improved to Medium on the latest evidence: TSMC's own VP describes capacity as now "very close" to demand after tripling-plus over three years, with yields above 98-99% in volume production. Because the strict qualification requires low elasticity alongside extreme demand and hard substitutability, this node no longer meets the bar as cleanly as it did in the prior research pass, and is reclassified here as a Growth Area pending confirmation at TSMC's Q3 2026 earnings call.

# Strongest Bear Case

- **Supply**: Multiple ABF substrate makers (Ibiden, Unimicron, and reportedly Shinko, AT&S, and China-based capacity such as Unimicron's Dingxin plant) are simultaneously investing in new capacity; if this capacity lands together over 2028-2030 as currently planned, the ABF shortage this research treats as structural could flip to oversupply, echoing prior memory and packaging cycles in this exact industry.
- **Technology**: TSMC is actively developing alternative, lower-cost packaging approaches (a pilot line targeted to mature within about a year) and glass-substrate technology; if these substitutes reach production sooner than expected, the "hard substitutability" finding for ABF-based substrates could weaken well before 2028.
- **Demand**: If AI hyperscaler capital-expenditure growth decelerates -- a risk already flagged in this pipeline's prior CoWoS and Nvidia-financing research -- demand growth for ABF film and substrates would soften in tandem, easing the classified bottleneck faster than current capacity-expansion timelines would suggest.
- **Competition**: Reported 30% price increases on adjacent substrate materials (copper-clad laminates from Resonac and Mitsubishi Gas Chemical) suggest cost pressure across the IC substrate supply chain is broader than a single-company monopoly story, which could indicate normal input-cost inflation across a tight but multi-supplier market rather than a uniquely concentrated chokepoint at Ajinomoto specifically.
- **Execution**: Available secondary reporting on Unimicron's Q2 2026 results contained internally inconsistent figures (one source cited a substantially different gross margin and EPS than the more detailed earnings-call summary used here), illustrating that the actual severity of margin and capacity strain across this supply chain is harder to pin down from public reporting than the cleaner narrative in this research suggests.

# Final Assessment

**Verdict: VALIDATE** (confidence: medium)

Verdict VALIDATE: the topic merits continued research with defined validation events rather than an immediate deep commitment. The evidence gathered supports a disciplined application of this pipeline's own bottleneck framework: rather than simply carrying forward the prior finding that CoWoS packaging is the AI accelerator supply chain's structural constraint, the latest evidence -- TSMC's own account of capacity nearly tripling over three years and closing in on demand -- argues for reclassifying CoWoS assembly as a Growth Area and relocating the binding constraint to ABF dielectric film and the substrates built from it, where Ajinomoto's near-monopoly, pricing power, and 2032 capacity timeline, together with Ibiden's and Unimicron's own multi-year, multi-billion-dollar expansion plans, meet the strict definition of a structural bottleneck. The clearest risk to this thesis is the one this exact industry has lived through before: simultaneous capacity additions from multiple suppliers landing together could turn today's shortage into oversupply, just as CoWoS capacity itself appears to be catching up to demand faster than some earlier assessments implied. The defined kill conditions and validation events -- particularly the next round of TSMC, Ibiden, and Unimicron earnings calls -- should clarify within one to two quarters whether the ABF bottleneck deepens, plateaus, or begins to resolve on the same multi-year track CoWoS did.
